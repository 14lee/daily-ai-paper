| Every Question Has Its Own Value: Reinforcement Learning with Explicit
  Human Values (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20187) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.20187))|  | The paper introduces Reinforcement Learning with Explicit Human Values (RLEV), a method that scales correctness-based rewards with human-defined prompt values to align Large Language Model optimization with quantifiable human priorities. The primary objective is to develop a reinforcement learning framework that moves beyond binary correctness rewards by incorporating explicit, non-uniform human values for each prompt, thereby optimizing for a value-weighted utility function rather than just the raw number of correct answers. RLEV extends Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by implementing a clipped, additive reward function, r(x, y) = (1 + min(a · v(x), 1)) · 1_correct(y), where v(x) is the normalized value of prompt x, to provide a stronger, yet stable, learning signal for higher-value tasks. RLEV consistently outperforms correctness-only baselines; for 32B models, it increased the average value-weighted accuracy from 59.5% to 62.3% while more than halving the average response length from 246.9 to 98.6 tokens. For AI practitioners in domains with verifiable outcomes and varying task importance, RLEV offers a direct method to align models with explicit priorities, resulting in systems that strategically allocate resources to more critical tasks and generate more efficient responses. |
