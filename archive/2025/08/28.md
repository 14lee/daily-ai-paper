| Beyond Transcription: Mechanistic Interpretability in ASR (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15882) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.15882))| Aviv Shamsian, Hilit Segev, Yael Segal-Feldman, AvivNavon, netag | This paper applies mechanistic interpretability techniques to uncover the internal dynamics of ASR models. The objective is to understand how acoustic and semantic information evolves across ASR layers and the mechanisms behind error phenomena like hallucinations and repetitions. The study systematically adapted and applied logit lens, linear probing, and activation patching, along with causal intervention methods such as component patching and ablation. Key findings include that hallucination-related signals are linearly decodable from the decoder's residual stream with up to 93.4% accuracy at layer 22 for Whisper, and that cross-attention patching at layer 23 resolved 76% of repetition cases. These insights provide AI practitioners with tools for building internal quality monitors, enabling targeted debugging, and informing architectural decisions for more reliable ASR systems. |
| Self-Rewarding Vision-Language Model via Reasoning Decomposition (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19652) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.19652))| Zhenwen Liang, Rui Liu, Wenhao Yu, Zongxia Li, ChengsongHuang | Vision-SR1 is a self-rewarding reinforcement learning framework that enhances VLM visual reasoning and reduces hallucinations by decomposing reasoning into self-validated visual perception and language reasoning. The main objective is to overcome visual hallucinations and language shortcuts in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by providing explicit guidance for intermediate visual reasoning without relying on external visual supervision. Vision-SR1 decomposes VLM reasoning into visual perception and language reasoning stages, generating a structured output with an accuracy reward in the first pass, and then re-prompting the VLM with only the generated visual perception to self-validate its faithfulness and assign a self-visual reward. Across diverse vision-language benchmarks, Vision-SR1 achieved an average accuracy of 58.8 with the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, outperforming Vision-R1's 57.4. AI practitioners can leverage this self-rewarding reasoning decomposition to develop more robust and visually grounded VLMs, mitigating hallucinations and shortcut learning without the need for costly external visual supervision. |

| Predicting the Order of Upcoming Tokens Improves Language Modeling (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19228) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.19228))| Alham Fikri Aji, Erland, zaydzuhri | Token Order Prediction (TOP) significantly improves language modeling by predicting the order of upcoming tokens. The research aimed to enhance next-token prediction (NTP) performance through a novel auxiliary objective, overcoming Multi-Token Prediction (MTP)'s limitation of exact future token prediction. TOP utilizes a learning-to-rank loss (ListNet-like) to order tokens by proximity within a window, requiring only a single additional unembedding layer. Across eight standard NLP benchmarks, TOP models (340M, 1.8B, 7B) consistently outperformed NTP and MTP; for instance, the 7B TOP model achieved 57.03% Lambada accuracy (NTP: 55.89%, MTP: 53.13%) and 30.90% TriviaQA exact match (NTP: 24.28%, MTP: 23.36%). | Diffusion Language Models Know the Answer Before Decoding (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19982) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.19982))| Shilin Yan, Lu Yin, Dilxat Muhtar, Yefan Zhou, Pengxiang Li | Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) exhibit an "early answer convergence" property, where correct answers can be internally identified by half the refinement steps. This paper aims to accelerate DLM inference by leveraging this phenomenon, addressing the challenge of DLMs being slower than autoregressive models due to numerous refinement steps. The authors introduce Prophet, a training-free fast decoding paradigm that dynamically decides whether to continue refinement or perform "early commit decoding" by monitoring a "Confidence Gap" metric (the difference between top-2 prediction candidates) with a time-varying risk aversion threshold. Empirical evaluations show Prophet reduces decoding steps by up to 3.4x (e.g., on Sudoku with Dream-7B) while preserving high generation quality, with up to 99% of instances (e.g., MMLU with random remasking) being correctly decoded within half the total steps. For AI practitioners, Prophet offers a robust, model-agnostic, and training-free method to significantly accelerate DLM inference, improving their practical deployment by reducing computational costs without compromising output quality. |

This highlights the urgent need for AI practitioners to develop robust, specialized privacy training, tighter alignment strategies, and dedicated evaluation benchmarks to enhance privacy protection in MLLM-powered agents. |
