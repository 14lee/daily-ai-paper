| Pref-GRPO: Pairwise Preference Reward-based GRPO for Stable
  Text-to-Image Reinforcement Learning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20751) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.20751))| Jiazi Bu, Yujie Zhou, Zhimin Li, yuhangzang, CodeGoat24 | The paper introduces PREF-GRPO, a pairwise preference-based reinforcement learning method to mitigate reward hacking and stabilize text-to-image model training. The research aims to solve the training instability in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) caused by "illusory advantage," where minimal differences in pointwise reward scores are disproportionately amplified after normalization, leading to reward hacking. The key methodology replaces pointwise reward maximization with pairwise preference fitting by calculating a "win rate" for each generated image based on pairwise comparisons from a preference reward model, using this rate as a more stable reward signal. Experiments show that PREF-GRPO improves the overall semantic consistency score on the new UNIGENBENCH benchmark by 5.84% (from 63.62 to 69.46) compared to the strongest pointwise reward-based GRPO baseline. For AI practitioners, this work implies that employing pairwise preference rewards over absolute scores during RL-based finetuning of generative models is a more robust strategy to prevent policy degradation from reward hacking and ensure stable performance improvements. |

The main objective is to overcome the quadratic computational and memory complexity of self-attention in diffusion transformers, which currently limits the practical generation of long video sequences. The key methodology involves partitioning the video token stream into content-aligned chunks (e.g., shots, frames) and using a dynamic, parameter-free top-k router for each query to select a small subset of relevant past chunks to attend to, alongside mandatory anchors like the global caption and local context. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that learned, dynamic sparse attention provides a computationally feasible path to scale generative video models to long-form content, effectively converting the attention bottleneck into a data-driven retrieval problem. |

| Provable Benefits of In-Tool Learning for Large Language Models (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20755) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.20755))| Vivien Cabannes, Charles Arnal, Ambroise Odonnat, Sam Houliston | This paper theoretically proves and empirically demonstrates that augmenting Large Language Models with external tools for factual recall is fundamentally more scalable than memorizing facts within model parameters. The research objective is to formalize the trade-off between "in-weight learning" (memorization) and "in-tool learning" (external retrieval) to determine the more efficient method for knowledge acquisition. The methodology combines theoretical analysis, deriving a parameter lower bound for memorization and a circuit construction for tool-use, with controlled experiments training transformers from scratch and fine-tuning pretrained LLMs (e.g., Llama 3) on synthetic factual datasets. The primary result shows that while the required model size for in-weight learning scales linearly with the number of facts, in-tool learning enables unbounded recall with a constant-sized model; for instance, fine-tuning a 1B parameter Llama model to memorize 50k facts caused its HellaSwag accuracy to degrade from ~60% to ~45%, a degradation not observed in the tool-using model. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that system design should prioritize modular, tool-augmented architectures over scaling monolithic models for knowledge storage, as externalizing facts preserves core capabilities, reduces training costs, and prevents catastrophic forgetting when incorporating new information. |
