Models including Transformers (GPTNeox), LSTMs, Mamba, and ARMT were trained and evaluated on a 1dCA-Reasoning benchmark with disjoint train/test rule sets, using Adaptive Computation Time (ACT), Reinforcement Learning (GRPO), and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to extend effective depth. The paper's objective is to examine why CVR is necessary, outline its major architectures and reasoning paradigms across five developmental stages, detail current benchmarks and evaluation metrics, and identify prevailing limitations and open challenges in the field. The authors reviewed over 260 papers from January 2023 to May 2025 across top AI venues, categorizing CVR's evolution into five stages: prompt-enhanced language-centric, tool-enhanced LLMs, tool-enhanced VLMs, chain-of-thought VLMs, and unified agentic VLM-based frameworks. | Limitations of Normalization in Attention Mechanism (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17821) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.17821))| Radu State, Tatiana Petrova, mbur, opensapce | This paper theoretically and empirically analyzes the intrinsic limitations of softmax normalization in attention mechanisms. It aims to formalize the capacity limits of softmax by investigating its impact on token separability, representation distance, and gradient stability, particularly in long-context settings. The study derives non-asymptotic bounds for representation distance (collapsing to zero when selected tokens N grow proportionally to context length L, with empirical critical-N growing sub-linearly at ~0.06L), geometric separability (saturating at ~80% distinguishable tokens), and Jacobian norm (scaling as 1/(4T)), which were validated using a pre-trained GPT-2 model. For AI practitioners, this implies that attention mechanisms' active set should be limited to a sub-linear function of context length (L≫k), attention entropy monitored for saturation, and length-aware or sparsity-inducing normalizers preferred over aggressive temperature scaling (T<0.1) for better stability and separability. The most impactful finding is that attention's discriminative power is capacity-limited, with geometric separability saturating at around 80% of selected tokens, necessitating alternative normalization strategies for robust long-context Transformer models. |
