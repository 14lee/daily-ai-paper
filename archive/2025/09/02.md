| PVPO: Pre-Estimated Value-Based Policy Optimization for Agentic
  Reasoning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21104) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.21104))| Yuewei Zhang, Penghong Zhao, Wenfeng Feng, Chuzhan, Nothing2Say | PVPO is a critic-free reinforcement learning algorithm that improves policy optimization by using a static, pre-estimated value baseline and a group sampling strategy to enhance training stability and efficiency. The primary objective is to overcome the high computational cost, training instability, and local optima issues inherent in group policy methods that rely on dynamic intra-group comparisons for advantage estimation. The core methodology involves using a fixed reference model to compute a static value estimate (`Vsta`) as a stable advantage anchor and implementing an offline data filtering process to prioritize high-gain samples. On multi-step retrieval datasets, PVPO achieves a 61.00% average accuracy, outperforming the GRPO baseline's 56.78%, and a case study shows it can achieve 97% of a fully-budgeted baseline's performance using less than 40% of the computational cost. For AI practitioners, PVPO provides a more resource-efficient framework for training agentic LLMs, enabling faster convergence and significant computational savings by stabilizing the training signal and reducing the need for extensive online rollouts. |
| T2R-bench: A Benchmark for Generating Article-Level Reports from Real
  World Industrial Tables (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19813) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.19813))| Yu Zhao, Sishi Xiong, Kaiwen Wei, Changzai Pan, Jie Zhang | This paper introduces T2R-bench, a new bilingual benchmark for the novel task of generating article-level reports from complex industrial tables. The main objective is to evaluate the capability of large language models (LLMs) to transform structured tabular data into comprehensive natural language reports, addressing a critical gap in industrial applications. The methodology involves constructing a dataset of 457 real-world industrial tables across 19 domains and developing a new evaluation framework with three criteria: Numerical Accuracy (NAC), Information Coverage (ICC), and General Evaluation (GEC). The primary result from evaluating 25 LLMs is that even the top-performing model, Deepseek-R1, achieves a suboptimal overall score of 62.71%, indicating significant room for improvement. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that current state-of-the-art LLMs are not yet capable of producing reliable and accurate article-level reports from diverse industrial tables, highlighting the need for specialized model development to meet enterprise requirements. |
| No Label Left Behind: A Unified Surface Defect Detection Model for all
  Supervision Regimes (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19060) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.19060))| Danijel Skočaj, MaticFuc, blaz-r | SuperSimpleNet is a unified, efficient discriminative model for surface defect detection that achieves state-of-the-art performance across unsupervised, weakly supervised, mixed, and fully supervised regimes. The main objective is to develop a single model that can leverage all available data annotations—from normal-only images to image-level and pixel-level labels—to overcome the constraints of methods designed for a single supervision paradigm. The methodology enhances the SimpleNet architecture with a novel latent-space synthetic anomaly generation process using masked Perlin noise, a dual-head design featuring an added classification head, and a conditional loss function that enables training with heterogeneous labels. The model achieves state-of-the-art results, including 98.0% AUROC on the fully supervised SensumSODF dataset and 98.3% AUROC on the unsupervised MVTec AD benchmark, with an inference time of 9.5 ms. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is the ability to deploy a single, high-speed model that adapts to dynamic industrial data environments, effectively using whichever labels are available and improving performance as annotation detail increases, thus eliminating the need for separate models for different supervision scenarios. |
| UI-Level Evaluation of ALLaM 34B: Measuring an Arabic-Centric LLM via
  HUMAIN Chat (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17378) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.17378))| Omartificial-Intelligence-Space | This paper presents a UI-level evaluation of the 34B-parameter, Arabic-centric ALLaM model via the HUMAIN Chat interface to assess its performance across modern standard Arabic, regional dialects, and safety. The study's objective was to quantify the model's linguistic and cultural alignment through a structured prompt pack. The methodology involved collecting 115 responses (23 prompts x 5 runs) and scoring them on a five-point Likert scale across five dimensions using three frontier LLM judges (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet-4). Primary results show high performance in code-switching and generation tasks (both averaging 4.92/5), but reveal significantly lower and uneven performance in dialectal fidelity (4.21/5). The principal implication for AI practitioners is that developing culturally aligned, non-English LLMs requires targeted collection of high-quality dialectal corpora and specialized finetuning to overcome performance imbalances and ensure authentic linguistic representation beyond formal language. |
| From reactive to cognitive: brain-inspired spatial intelligence for
  embodied agents (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17198) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.17198))| Songming Liu, Qihui Zhu, Caixin Kang, Liyuan Wang, Shouwei Ruan | This paper introduces Brain-inspired Spatial Cognition for Navigation (BSC-Nav), a unified framework enabling embodied agents to construct and leverage structured spatial memory for cognitive, goal-directed behavior. The primary objective is to develop a system that explicitly builds an internal spatial model comprising landmarks, route knowledge, and survey knowledge to overcome the generalization and adaptability limitations of reactive, stateless agents. The key methodology involves a dual-branch architecture with a landmark memory module for salient cues and a cognitive map module to build allocentric representations from egocentric trajectories, integrated with multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) for high-level reasoning. The framework demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization, achieving a 38.5% Success Rate on the challenging long-horizon VLN-CE R2R benchmark without task-specific supervised training. For AI practitioners, BSC-Nav provides a scalable, biologically-grounded architecture that complements MLLMs' reasoning with cognitive spatial intelligence, enabling the development of more capable and adaptable embodied AI for complex, real-world environments. |
| How Can Input Reformulation Improve Tool Usage Accuracy in a Complex
  Dynamic Environment? A Study on τ-bench (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20931) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.20931))| Jayanth Srinivasa, Mutsumi Nakamura, Satyam Raj, Amir Saeidi, Venkatesh Mishra | This paper introduces the Input-Reformulation Multi-Agent (IRMA) framework to enhance the tool-use accuracy of LLM agents by augmenting user queries with domain rules and tool suggestions. The main objective is to mitigate common agent failure modes, such as policy violations and contextual misinterpretation, by improving the quality of the input provided to the tool-calling agent in dynamic, multi-turn environments. The key methodology is a multi-agent framework that reformulates user prompts to include conversation history (<memory>), relevant domain policies (<constraints>), and a focused list of applicable tools (<tool_suggested>) before the main agent makes a decision. The primary result is that IRMA significantly improves reliability, outperforming ReAct, Function Calling, and Self-Reflection by 16.1%, 12.7%, and 19.1% respectively in overall pass^5 scores on the τ-bench benchmark. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that pre-processing agent inputs to explicitly include structured domain knowledge and tool context is a highly effective, verification-loop-free method for improving the robustness and decision-making accuracy of autonomous agents in complex, stateful tasks. |
