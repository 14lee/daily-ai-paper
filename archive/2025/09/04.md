| Robix: A Unified Model for Robot Interaction, Reasoning and Planning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01106) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.01106))| Zixuan Wang, Wei Li, Heng Dong, Mengxi Zhang, Huang Fang | Robix is a unified vision-language model integrating robot reasoning, task planning, and natural language interaction within a single architecture. The primary objective is to develop a single, end-to-end cognitive model for hierarchical robot systems that handles complex instructions and dynamic human interaction, overcoming the limitations of modular pipelines. The methodology uses a three-stage training strategy: continued pretraining on a 200B token dataset for embodied reasoning, supervised finetuning on synthesized interaction data using chain-of-thought, and reinforcement learning with a thought-action consistency reward. Robix outperforms leading baselines; on a curated out-of-distribution (OOD) interactive-task benchmark, Robix-32B achieved 86.8% accuracy, surpassing the Gemini-2.5-Pro baseline by 3.0 percentage points. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that this unified, multi-stage training approach produces a more robust and generalizable high-level robot controller than multi-component systems, demonstrating a viable path for creating flexible, general-purpose robots that can handle novel, interactive tasks. |
| Open Data Synthesis For Deep Research (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00375) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.00375))| Zheng Liu, Hongjin Qian, Kun Luo, ZiyiXia | InfoSeek introduces an open-source data synthesis framework and dataset for training LLMs on complex Deep Research tasks formalized as Hierarchical Constraint Satisfaction Problems (HCSPs). The paper aims to fill the gap of high-quality, large-scale datasets for Deep Research, which demands decomposing questions into sub-problems, coordinating multi-step reasoning, and synthesizing evidence from diverse sources. InfoSeek's methodology uses a dual-agent system to recursively build Research Trees from large-scale webpages, converting these into natural language questions, and employs supervised fine-tuning with rejection sampling and reinforcement learning for model optimization. InfoSeeker-3B, an LLM trained with InfoSeek, achieved 16.5% accuracy on the BrowseComp-Plus benchmark, outperforming Qwen3-32B (3.5%) and lightweight commercial APIs like Gemini 2.5 Flash (15.5%). This provides AI practitioners with a scalable, open-source method to generate structurally complex and verifiable datasets, critical for developing more robust and efficient compact LLMs capable of advanced deep research. |
| LMEnt: A Suite for Analyzing Knowledge in Language Models from
  Pretraining Data to Representations (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03405) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.03405))| Yoav Gur-Arieh, Ido Cohen, Alon Gilae-Dotan, Daniela Gottesman, mega | LMEnt is a novel suite comprising knowledge-rich pretraining data with fine-grained entity annotations, an entity-based retrieval system, and a collection of pretrained language models for analyzing knowledge acquisition. The main objective is to understand how knowledge representations are formed and shaped during pretraining, specifically investigating the interplay between data composition, training dynamics, and internal knowledge mechanisms in LMs. The methodology involves a 3.6B token English Wikipedia corpus annotated with 400M entity mentions (using hyperlinks, entity linking, and coreference resolution), an Elasticsearch index for QID-based retrieval over 10.5M data chunks, and 12 OLMO-2 architecture-based models (170M, 600M, 1B parameters) trained for up to 6 epochs with 4K intermediate checkpoints. LMEnt's entity-based retrieval outperforms string-based methods by as much as 80.4% in relevant chunk retrieval while maintaining >97% precision. This suite provides AI practitioners with a controlled environment and tools to precisely track and analyze how LMs acquire, represent, and forget factual knowledge, facilitating advancements in knowledge plasticity, editing, attribution, and improving model factuality. |
| Mixture of Global and Local Experts with Diffusion Transformer for
  Controllable Face Generation (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00428) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.00428))| Kai Li, Yue Li, Xing Fu, Shun Zhang, Xuechao Zou | Face-MoGLE is a Diffusion Transformer-based framework for controllable face generation using a Mixture of Global and Local Experts. It addresses challenges in semantic controllability and photorealism by decoupling semantic masks and employing a diffusion-aware dynamic gating network for adaptive expert blending. The methodology integrates global experts for holistic structure and local experts for fine-grained details, leveraging LoRA for efficient fine-tuning. Face-MoGLE achieved state-of-the-art multimodal face generation on MM-CelebA-HQ, with FID of 22.24, KID of 10.87, CMMD of 0.477, mask alignment of 2.44, and text alignment of 26.32. This work demonstrates a robust solution for high-quality, controllable face generation, offering AI practitioners a scalable and semantically-aware architectural paradigm for generative modeling. |
| MOSAIC: Multi-Subject Personalized Generation via Correspondence-Aware
  Alignment and Disentanglement (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01977) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.01977))| Hualiang Wang, Qiaoqiao Jin, Mushui Liu, Siming Fu, Dong She | MOSAIC is a representation-centric framework for multi-subject personalized image generation that leverages explicit semantic correspondence and orthogonal feature disentanglement. The primary objective is to overcome identity blending, attribute leakage, and degradation in multi-subject generation by enabling precise semantic alignment and preventing feature interference, especially with 4+ reference subjects. MOSAIC introduces the SemAlign-MS dataset for fine-grained semantic correspondences and employs a semantic correspondence attention loss (LSCA) to enforce point-to-point alignment, alongside a multi-reference disentanglement loss (LMD) to push subject representations into orthogonal attention subspaces using KL divergence. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance, notably improving multi-subject generation on DreamBench with a CLIP-I score of 76.30 and demonstrating high fidelity with 4+ reference subjects, where existing methods typically degrade. AI practitioners can apply MOSAIC's principles of explicit correspondence supervision and attention disentanglement to develop more robust and scalable multi-subject generative models, enhancing control and fidelity in complex image synthesis applications. |
