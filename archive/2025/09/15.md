| IntrEx: A Dataset for Modeling Engagement in Educational Conversations (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06652) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.06652))| Gabriele Pergola, Chiara Gambi, Mahathi Parvatham, XingweiT | This paper introduces IntrEx, the first large-scale dataset annotated for interestingness and expected interestingness in educational teacher-student conversations. The research objective is to identify the linguistic features that drive conversational engagement and to assess the capability of Large Language Models (LLMs) to predict these human judgments. The authors collected sequence-level annotations from over 100 second-language learners on the TSCC corpus using a comparison-based rating approach inspired by RLHF and used this data to fine-tune LLMs. The primary result is that fine-tuned 7B/8B parameter models outperform larger proprietary models; for instance, Mistral-7B-Instruct achieved an AC2 agreement score of 0.5142 in predicting human interest, surpassing GPT-4o's score of 0.4657. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that specialized, high-quality datasets enable smaller, fine-tuned models to achieve superior performance on nuanced, subjective tasks like engagement modeling, providing an effective alternative to larger, general-purpose models for developing specialized conversational AI. |
| The Illusion of Diminishing Returns: Measuring Long Horizon Execution in
  LLMs (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09677) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.09677))| Jonas Geiping, Steffen Staab, Shashwat Goel, arvindh75, viciousa3gis | This paper argues that diminishing returns from scaling LLMs are an illusion if measured by long-horizon execution capability, where small single-step accuracy gains compound exponentially. The research aims to isolate and measure the pure execution capability of LLMs, decoupled from planning and knowledge, to understand failure modes in long tasks. A synthetic key-value dictionary task is used where models maintain a running sum over many turns, with the plan (keys) and knowledge (dictionary) provided in-context. The primary results show that performance degrades due to a "self-conditioning" effect, where models become more likely to err after observing their own past mistakes, an issue not solved by model scaling alone; however, sequential test-time compute ("thinking") eliminates this effect, with GPT-5 executing over 1000 steps in a single turn compared to 432 for the next-best model. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that for reliable long-horizon agentic systems, enabling sequential reasoning (like chain-of-thought) is more critical than just scaling model size, as it directly mitigates execution degradation over time. |
| InfGen: A Resolution-Agnostic Paradigm for Scalable Image Synthesis (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10441) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.10441))| Song Guo, Xiaoyu Yue, Junchao Gong, Wanghan Xu, Tao Han | InfGen is a resolution-agnostic generator that replaces the VAE decoder in latent diffusion models to enable fast, arbitrary-resolution image synthesis from a fixed-size latent without retraining the diffusion model. The primary objective is to address the high computational cost and latency of generating high-resolution images with diffusion models by decoupling the content generation from the resolution rendering process. The key methodology involves a transformer-based decoder that uses a fixed latent from a diffusion model as a key/value prompt and a dynamically sized query token with Implicit Neural Positional Embeddings (INPE) to generate an image of any target resolution in a single forward pass. Experiments show InfGen significantly reduces inference time, generating 4K images in under 10 seconds, and improves generation quality, achieving a 41% FIDp improvement for the DiT-XL/2 model when generating 3072x3072 images. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that InfGen can be used as a plug-and-play module to upgrade existing diffusion models like Stable Diffusion for arbitrary high-resolution generation, providing a computationally efficient solution without altering the original models. |
| X-Part: high fidelity and structure coherent shape decomposition (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08643) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.08643))| Yunhan Yang, Changfeng Ma, Yang Li, Jiachen Xu, HowieYan | X-Part is a controllable, diffusion-based generative model that decomposes holistic 3D objects into high-fidelity, semantically meaningful, and structurally coherent parts. The primary objective is to develop a method that can decompose a given 3D object into its constituent parts with high geometric fidelity and semantic coherence, while providing user controllability over the decomposition process. The model utilizes a multi-part latent diffusion framework conditioned on bounding boxes and injected point-wise semantic features from a P3-SAM segmenter, employing a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with both intra-part and inter-part self-attention to generate all part latents simultaneously. X-Part achieves state-of-the-art performance in 3D shape decomposition, demonstrating a Chamfer Distance of 0.11 and an F-score (at threshold 0.1) of 0.80 on the ObjaversePart-Tiny dataset. For AI practitioners, this work provides a production-ready framework for generating editable 3D assets, where direct control via bounding box manipulation simplifies downstream tasks like UV unwrapping and mesh retopology in content creation pipelines. |
| HANRAG: Heuristic Accurate Noise-resistant Retrieval-Augmented
  Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09713) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.09713))| Zhehao Tan, Yihan Jiao, Yue Shen, Dan Yang, Duolin Sun | HANRAG is a novel Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework utilizing a heuristic-based master agent, 'Revelator,' to efficiently manage diverse multi-hop question-answering tasks. The research objective is to overcome the inefficiency, irrational querying, and noise accumulation prevalent in current multi-hop RAG systems by creating an adaptive framework that differentiates between simple, compound, and complex queries. The key methodology is centered on the 'Revelator' agent, which performs four primary functions: routing queries to appropriate processing chains, decomposing compound queries for parallel asynchronous retrieval, iteratively refining sub-questions for complex queries, and discriminating the relevance of retrieved documents to filter noise. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that designing RAG systems with a central orchestrating agent that can classify query types and apply specialized, modular processing paths (e.g., parallel vs. serial retrieval) significantly enhances both accuracy and computational efficiency. |

The main objective is to assess whether SLMs can modify acoustic and paralinguistic features like timbre, prosody, and persona in response to natural language commands. The methodology consists of the VStyle dataset, comprising 1,523 prompts across four categories, and an automated evaluation pipeline using a Large Audio Language Model to hierarchically score outputs on content, style, and naturalness. Results show a significant performance gap, with commercial models like GPT-4o (4.05 overall score in English) outperforming open-source models, and the automated evaluation achieving a high correlation with human consensus (77.01% Spearman correlation for English). The principal implication for AI practitioners is the provision of a public, scalable, and reproducible toolkit to benchmark and enhance the expressive, controllable capabilities of speech generation systems beyond semantic accuracy. |
| FLOWER: Democratizing Generalist Robot Policies with Efficient

The research aims to create a generalist robot policy with sub-billion parameters to reduce the prohibitive computational costs of existing VLA models. The methodology integrates intermediate-modality fusion, which prunes 30-50% of the VLM's final layers, with an action-specific Global-AdaLN conditioned Flow Transformer to handle diverse action spaces efficiently. FLOWER establishes a new state-of-the-art performance of 4.53 average sequence length on the CALVIN ABC benchmark and doubles the real-world success rate of OpenVLA (61% vs. 31%). The principal implication for AI practitioners is the demonstration of a viable architectural blueprint for developing high-performance, generalist robotic policies with significantly lower computational and memory footprints, making them more practical for real-world deployment on commodity hardware. |
| Inpainting-Guided Policy Optimization for Diffusion Large Language
  Models (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10396) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.10396))| Chenyu Wang, Miao Liu, Jing Huang, Mengchen Liu, Siyan Zhao | This paper introduces Inpainting-Guided Policy Optimization (IGPO), a reinforcement learning framework that leverages the unique inpainting capabilities of masked diffusion large language models (dLLMs) to enhance their mathematical reasoning. The primary objective is to mitigate the exploration challenge in RL, where sparse rewards lead to inefficient training, by using partial ground-truth hints to guide the model. The key methodology involves strategically injecting segments of correct reasoning traces into the generation context when all sampled responses are incorrect, prompting the dLLM to complete the solution via inpainting and thus restoring a learning signal for group-based policy optimization. The full training recipe achieves state-of-the-art performance for full-attention masked dLLMs, attaining an 86.4% pass@1 score on GSM8K, a +4.9% absolute improvement over the LLaDA-Instruct baseline. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that a model's architectural features, like inpainting in dLLMs, can be directly exploited to design more sample-efficient and effective RL algorithms, providing a practical solution to the common "zero-advantage" problem in policy optimization for complex reasoning tasks. |

The central objective is to propose proactive design choices for this economy—including auction mechanisms for resource allocation, "mission economies" for collective goals, and socio-technical infrastructure—to mitigate the risks of a spontaneously emergent and highly permeable system. The methodology is a conceptual analysis that characterizes agent economies along two dimensions (emergent vs. intentional origins; permeable vs. impermeable boundaries) and synthesizes principles from economics, multi-agent systems, and blockchain technology. | QuantAgent: Price-Driven Multi-Agent LLMs for High-Frequency Trading (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09995) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.09995))| Chenyu You, Siqi Sun, Aosong Feng, Xiang Zhang, Fei Xiong | The paper introduces QuantAgent, a multi-agent LLM framework for high-frequency trading that operates exclusively on price data. The research objective is to create a specialized multi-agent LLM framework for high-frequency trading that can make rapid, risk-aware decisions from structured OHLC price data, avoiding the latency and noise of textual news or sentiment inputs. The key methodology involves decomposing the trading task into four specialized agents—Indicator, Pattern, Trend, and Risk—each equipped with domain-specific tools to analyze distinct aspects of market dynamics, with their outputs integrated by a final DecisionAgent. In zero-shot evaluations across ten financial instruments, the framework demonstrated superior performance over baselines; for example, on the SPX index, QuantAgent achieved 62.0% directional accuracy, a 59.0% improvement over the random baseline, and converted a negative average return to a positive one. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that complex, high-frequency decision-making tasks can be effectively addressed by a multi-agent LLM architecture where specialized agents are augmented with domain-specific tools, demonstrating how to integrate structured priors with language-native reasoning for traceable, real-time systems. |

The objective is to address the overconfidence and poor pseudo-label quality of traditional LTSSL models by leveraging the inherent calibration and generalization of pre-trained transformers. The methodology involves applying Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) to a foundation model to generate reliable pseudo-labels, with LoFT-OW incorporating a two-stage filtering mechanism to detect and remove out-of-distribution (OOD) samples from the unlabeled data. | MCP-AgentBench: Evaluating Real-World Language Agent Performance with

The primary objective is to address the critical evaluation gap where existing benchmarks fail to measure agent effectiveness within the standardized MCP framework, leading to a distorted perception of real-world capabilities. The methodology consists of establishing a testbed with 33 MCP servers and 188 tools, creating a dataset of 600 queries across six complexity categories, and developing an outcome-oriented, LLM-as-a-judge evaluation framework named MCP-Eval. The primary result shows that leading open-source models can outperform proprietary ones, with Qwen3-235B-A22B (using ReAct) achieving the highest overall pass rate of 64.7%, surpassing the top proprietary model, Claude 4 Sonnet (using TC), which scored 58.0%. | CMHG: A Dataset and Benchmark for Headline Generation of Minority
  Languages in China (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09990) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.09990))| XU Han, Jianing Liu, Ziyin Zhang, Zeli Su, Guixian Xu | This paper introduces CMHG, a large-scale dataset and benchmark for headline generation in three low-resource Chinese minority languages: Tibetan, Uyghur, and Mongolian. The primary objective is to address the data scarcity for supervised NLP tasks in these languages by providing a novel, open-source corpus and a high-quality, human-annotated test set. The methodology involved crawling web data to create a dataset of 100,000 Tibetan and 50,000 each of Uyghur and Mongolian samples, followed by native-speaker annotation of a subset to establish a reliable benchmark for evaluating both fine-tuned and few-shot models. Experiments show that large language models perform strongly in a few-shot setting, with LLaMA3.1-70B achieving a ROUGE-L F1 score of 0.34 on the high-quality Tibetan and Uyghur test sets. For AI practitioners, CMHG provides a crucial resource for fine-tuning and benchmarking models in these languages, demonstrating that few-shot learning with large models is a viable approach when extensive annotated training data is unavailable. |
