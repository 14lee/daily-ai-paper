| A Survey of Reinforcement Learning for Large Reasoning Models (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08827) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.08827))| Runze Liu, Youbang Sun, Bingxiang He, Yuxin Zuo, Kaiyan Zhang | This paper surveys the application of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) to transform large language models into advanced reasoning models. The objective is to systematically review the foundational components, core problems, training resources, and applications of RL for reasoning to identify scalable future directions. Through a comprehensive literature review, the survey identifies a paradigm shift from alignment-focused RLHF to capability-enhancing RLVR using automated, objective rewards, citing that the Afterburner model improved its Pass@1 rate on coding tasks from 47% to 62% via RL. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that for verifiable domains like coding and mathematics, implementing RLVR with automated rewards and critic-free optimizers provides a more scalable and robust path to improving reasoning capabilities than traditional human-preference-based alignment methods. |
| RewardDance: Reward Scaling in Visual Generation (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08826) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.08826))| Liang Li, Ming Li, Zilyu Ye, Yu Gao, Jie Wu | i) This paper introduces RewardDance, a scalable reward modeling framework for visual generation that uses a generative paradigm to mitigate reward hacking and improve output quality. ii) The research objective is to develop a scalable reward model for visual generation that overcomes the architectural and paradigm limitations of prior methods, which are often susceptible to reward hacking and do not scale effectively. iii) The key methodology involves reformulating the reward score as a Vision-Language Model's (VLM) predicted probability of generating a "yes" token for a comparative judgment, enabling scaling across two dimensions: model size (from 1B to 26B parameters) and context richness (task-instructions, reference images, CoT). iv) Primary results demonstrate that scaling the reward model consistently improves generation; for instance, applying the 26B RewardDance model via RL fine-tuning increased the Seedream-3.0 model's alignment score from a 74.1 baseline to 84.8. v) The principal implication for AI practitioners is that scaling reward models is as critical as scaling generation models, and adopting a generative reward paradigm offers a robust method to prevent reward hacking and unlock higher performance in RLHF pipelines for visual generation. |
| 3D and 4D World Modeling: A Survey (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07996) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.07996))| Ao Liang, Youquan Liu, Jianbiao Mei, Wesley Yang, Lingdong Kong | This survey presents the first comprehensive review of 3D and 4D world modeling, establishing a structured taxonomy for methods that leverage native 3D/4D representations like LiDAR and occupancy grids. The primary objective is to address the absence of a standardized definition and unified framework for 3D/4D world models, which are critical for safety-critical systems but are often overlooked in favor of 2D approaches. The paper's methodology involves categorizing existing literature into three main approaches based on data modality—VideoGen (video-based), OccGen (occupancy-based), and LiDARGen (LiDAR-based)—and four functional types, including Data Engines and Neural Simulators. Through systematic benchmarking, the survey reports that advanced occupancy reconstruction models like X-Scene achieve state-of-the-art performance, reaching 92.40% mIoU on the nuScenes dataset. For AI practitioners, this work provides a foundational reference with standardized definitions, datasets, and evaluation metrics essential for developing, selecting, and applying world models in fields such as autonomous driving and robotics. |
| AgentGym-RL: Training LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Decision Making
  through Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08755) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.08755))| Honglin Guo, Baodai Huang, Chenyang Liao, Jixuan Huang, Zhiheng Xi | This paper introduces AgentGym-RL, a framework for training LLM agents from scratch using multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) for long-horizon decision-making.  The main objective is to develop a unified, interactive RL framework that can effectively train LLM agents across diverse, realistic environments without relying on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as a preliminary step.  The key methodology involves the AgentGym-RL framework, which has a modular architecture separating the agent, environment, and training modules, and supports mainstream RL algorithms like PPO and GRPO. The authors also propose ScalingInter-RL, a curriculum-based training approach that progressively increases the maximum number of interaction turns, balancing the exploration-exploitation trade-off.  The primary result is that open-source models trained with this framework and method demonstrate significant performance gains, with a 7B model achieving an average improvement of 33.65 points across five task scenarios, matching or surpassing larger commercial models like OpenAI-o3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro.  The principal implication for AI practitioners is that strategic post-training with progressive, multi-turn RL can elevate the performance of smaller open-source LLMs to levels competitive with much larger proprietary models, suggesting that interaction-based training is a more effective scaling vector than parameter count alone for developing agentic intelligence. |
| P3-SAM: Native 3D Part Segmentation (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06784) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.06784))| Yunhan Yang, Jiachen Xu, Xinhao Yan, Yang Li, murcherful | P3-SAM is a native 3D point-promptable part segmentation model that automates the decomposition of any 3D object into its constituent components. The research objective is to create a fully automatic 3D-native segmentation system that circumvents the imprecision and robustness issues inherent in methods that lift 2D segmentation results to 3D. The methodology utilizes a Point Transformer V3 feature extractor, a two-stage multi-head segmentor that predicts multi-scale masks from a single point prompt, and an IoU predictor to select the optimal mask, all trained on a novel dataset of 3.7 million 3D models. P3-SAM achieves state-of-the-art results, scoring an average Intersection over Union (IOU) of 81.14% on the PartObjectarverse-Tiny benchmark for segmentation with connectivity. For AI practitioners, this work provides a highly accurate and robust framework for direct, automated 3D asset decomposition, enabling advanced applications in part-based generation and interactive model editing without reliance on 2D data engines. |
| Hunyuan-MT Technical Report (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05209) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.05209))| Yang Du, Mingyang Song, Bingxin Qu, Zheng Li, Mao Zheng | This paper introduces Hunyuan-MT-7B and Hunyuan-MT-Chimera-7B, open-source multilingual translation models with 7B parameters. The objective was to develop effective LLM-based multilingual translation systems, emphasizing Mandarin and ethnic minority languages, and to bridge the performance gap between proprietary and open-source models. The methodology involves a holistic training process including general and MT-oriented pre-training, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and a novel weak-to-strong RL stage for Hunyuan-MT-Chimera-7B. The models achieved state-of-the-art performance, ranking first in 30 out of 31 WMT2025 language pairs, with Hunyuan-MT-7B scoring an XCOMET-XXL of 0.8585 on WMT24pp (outperforming Gemini-2.5-Pro at 0.8250) and Hunyuan-MT-Chimera-7B showing a ~4.7% relative improvement over Gemini-2.5-Pro on Mandarin⇔Minority translation. These models offer accessible, high-performance open-source foundation models for multilingual machine translation, particularly beneficial for low-resource and minority languages, demonstrating the effectiveness of task-specific optimization and weak-to-strong fusion for enhanced quality. |
| <think> So let's replace this phrase with insult... </think> Lessons
  learned from generation of toxic texts with LLMs (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08358) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.08358))| Alexander Panchenko, Daniil Moskovskiy, Sergey Pletenev | The paper explores the viability of using LLM-generated synthetic toxic data as an alternative to human-annotated data for training text detoxification models. Its main objective was to determine if LLMs could fully replace human annotators for generating toxic language in parallel datasets for detoxification. The methodology involved generating synthetic toxic texts from neutral sources (ParaDetox, SST-2) using activation-patched LLMs (Llama 3, Qwen3, Cogito v1) with min_p sampling, then fine-tuning BART-large models on this data and evaluating them against a human-annotated baseline. Results showed that models trained on synthetic data consistently underperformed human-trained models, exhibiting a performance drop of up to 30% in joint metrics, with the most severe being a -0.159 ∆(J) for Llama 3 8B generated from SST-2 data, primarily due to LLMs' limited lexical diversity in generating toxic expressions. Therefore, AI practitioners should be aware that exclusive reliance on LLM-generated toxic data for detoxification training results in less robust and generalizable models, highlighting the continued necessity of diverse human-annotated data. |
| EnvX: Agentize Everything with Agentic AI (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08088) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.08088))| Wenzheng Tom Tang, Yikun Wang, Yingxuan Yang, Zimian Peng, Linyao Chen | EnvX is a novel framework that agentizes GitHub repositories, transforming them into intelligent, autonomous agents for natural language interaction and inter-agent collaboration. The primary objective of EnvX is to enable users to directly invoke existing repository functionalities through natural language instructions and facilitate multi-agent collaboration for complex tasks within the software ecosystem. This is achieved via a three-phase process: (1) TODO-guided environment initialization, (2) human-aligned agentic automation using a meta-agent framework and structured tool integration, and (3) an Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocol for communication among repository agents. Evaluated on the GitTaskBench benchmark, EnvX achieved a 74.07% execution completion rate and a 51.85% task pass rate with Claude 3.7 Sonnet, outperforming existing frameworks. This approach shifts repositories from passive code resources to interactive agents, directly enabling AI practitioners to automate repository understanding, initialization, and operationalization via natural language, fostering greater accessibility and collaboration in open-source development. |
| HumanAgencyBench: Scalable Evaluation of Human Agency Support in AI
  Assistants (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08494) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.08494))| Jacy Reese Anthis, Jacob Haimes, Daniel Samuelson, Benjamin Sturgeon | This paper introduces HUMANAGENCYBENCH (HAB), a scalable benchmark to evaluate how large language model (LLM) assistants support human agency across six distinct dimensions. The research objective is to operationalize philosophical and scientific theories of agency into a systematic evaluation framework by measuring an assistant's tendency to Ask Clarifying Questions, Avoid Value Manipulation, Correct Misinformation, Defer Important Decisions, Encourage Learning, and Maintain Social Boundaries. The methodology uses an LLM-driven pipeline to simulate user queries, validate their quality, and evaluate assistant responses against a deduction-based rubric for each of the six dimensions. Primary results show low-to-moderate agency support across contemporary LLMs, with Anthropic's models scoring highest overall (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 54.6) but performing worst on the Avoid Value Manipulation dimension. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that current post-training objectives like instruction-following (e.g., via RLHF) may be in tension with human agency support, indicating a need to develop more robust safety and alignment targets. |
