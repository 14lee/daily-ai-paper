| OmniWorld: A Multi-Domain and Multi-Modal Dataset for 4D World Modeling (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12201) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.12201))| Yang Zhou, MingyuLiu, Xxxy13, lizizun, ZhouTimeMachine | This paper introduces OmniWorld, a large-scale, multi-domain, and multi-modal dataset designed to advance 4D world modeling. The work aims to address the data scarcity for training general 4D world models by providing a comprehensive resource with dynamic complexity and rich spatio-temporal annotations. The methodology involves collecting a new synthetic dataset from game environments (OmniWorld-Game, >18M frames) and curating several public datasets, which are then processed through an annotation pipeline for modalities like depth, camera poses, optical flow, and text. The primary result shows that fine-tuning existing models on OmniWorld yields significant performance gains; for example, a fine-tuned DUSt3R model reduced the Absolute Relative error for monocular depth estimation on the Sintel benchmark from 0.488 to 0.370. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that OmniWorld serves as a powerful training and evaluation resource for developing more robust 3D geometric reconstruction and camera-controlled video generation models that can better generalize to complex, dynamic scenes. |
| UI-S1: Advancing GUI Automation via Semi-online Reinforcement Learning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.11543))| Yongliang Shen, Fei Tang, xhyandwyy, Mizukiluke, LZXzju | The paper introduces UI-S1, a GUI automation agent trained with Semi-online Reinforcement Learning, a paradigm that simulates online interaction on static offline trajectories to improve multi-turn task performance. The primary objective is to bridge the gap between stable but short-sighted offline RL and effective but costly online RL for training GUI agents. The key methodology involves performing rollouts on static trajectories, maintaining the agent's self-generated history, and using a "Patch Module" to recover from action mismatches by injecting expert data, while optimizing the policy with discounted future returns and dual-level (step and episode) advantages. The resulting UI-S1-7B model achieves state-of-the-art performance among 7B models, with a +12.0% success rate increase on AndroidWorld and +23.8% on AITW over the base model. For AI practitioners, this work provides a framework for training robust, multi-turn capable GUI agents using only offline datasets, significantly reducing the cost and complexity associated with live environment interaction, while also offering a more reliable proxy metric (SOP) for real-world evaluation. |

The objective is to overcome the instability and limitations of prior drag-based editing methods, which stem from their reliance on implicit point matching via attention, by developing a technique that allows for stable, full-strength inversion without test-time optimization (TTO). The method generates an explicit correspondence map from user-defined handle and target points using a winner-takes-all (WTA) strategy to resolve conflicting drags, which then drives a two-part attention control mechanism involving token replacement for background preservation and token concatenation with gated output merging for identity preservation. | Locality in Image Diffusion Models Emerges from Data Statistics (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09672) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.09672))| Vincent Sitzmann, Justin Solomon, Chenyang Yuan, Artem Lukoianov | This research demonstrates that the locality in diffusion models is not an architectural inductive bias but a statistical property emerging from pixel correlations within the training data. The main objective is to determine whether the locality observed in deep diffusion models stems from architectural inductive biases or from the statistical properties of the image dataset itself. The study analyzes the dataset's principal components and connects them to the Wiener filter (optimal linear denoiser), showing that its sensitivity fields align with those learned by diverse architectures like UNets and Transformers; this is validated by manipulating dataset covariance to induce specific sensitivity patterns in a trained model. On the CelebA-HQ dataset, the proposed analytical denoiser, which incorporates locality derived from data statistics, explains the predictions of a trained DDPM with an r² score of 0.902±0.032, outperforming prior analytical models based on architectural assumptions (r²=0.795). The principal implication for AI practitioners is that a model's denoising behavior is primarily dictated by the training data's second-order statistics, meaning practitioners can analyze dataset covariance to predict and potentially engineer a model's sensitivity fields, independent of architectural choices like convolutions. |

The key methodology is a new 22,831-question dataset, derived from panoptic scene graphs and refined through manual filtering, where each multiple-choice question includes a mandatory "None of the above" option. The primary results demonstrate a critical failure in epistemic humility: the top-performing model, GLM-4.1V-Thinking, achieved 73.46% accuracy overall but scored only 0.06% on a stress test where abstention was always the correct choice. The methodology involves two approaches: calculating the k-nearest neighbors overlap ratio (KNOR) to assess changes in latent geometric structure, and training a reconstruction model to quantify and pinpoint patch-level information loss. The primary results demonstrate that connectors cause substantial distortion, with k-nearest neighbors diverging by 40-60% post-projection, correlating with performance degradation on retrieval tasks. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that high patch-level reconstruction loss reliably predicts model failures on visually grounded tasks, offering an interpretable method for diagnosing VLM weaknesses. |

| Look Again, Think Slowly: Enhancing Visual Reflection in Vision-Language

The primary objective is to solve the problem where existing Vision Reasoning Models (VRMs) rapidly lose attention to visual information during long-form text generation, thereby degrading their reasoning capabilities. The methodology employs a two-stage process: first, a cold-start initialization using reasoning data generated by an interactive LLM-VLM agent, followed by reinforcement learning (GRPO) with a novel visual attention-based reward that encourages sustained focus on visual tokens. The resulting Reflection-V-7B model achieves a score of 73.3% on MathVista and 61.3% on MMMU, significantly outperforming its base model which scored 68.2% and 54.3% on the same benchmarks. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that building robust VRMs for complex tasks requires explicit mechanisms to maintain visual grounding throughout the reasoning process, and the proposed interaction-based data construction and attention-based reward offer a concrete framework to achieve this. |

| Learning to Optimize Multi-Objective Alignment Through Dynamic Reward
  Weighting (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11452) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.11452))| Changlong Yu, Xin Liu, Shiyang Li, Zilong Wang, ylu610 | This paper introduces dynamic reward weighting for multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) in large language models (LLMs) to overcome limitations of static scalarization. The objective is to achieve superior Pareto fronts in LLM alignment by adaptively adjusting reward weights, addressing the failure of fixed-weight linear scalarization to capture non-convex Pareto regions. Two approaches are proposed: hypervolume-guided weight adaptation, which rewards new non-dominated solutions, and gradient-based weight optimization, which dynamically reallocates weights based on objective learning contributions derived from policy gradients. Experiments show that dynamic reward weighting consistently yields Pareto dominant solutions with fewer training steps; specifically, the gradient-based method reduced average training steps by 6.1 across online RL algorithms compared to baselines. AI practitioners should leverage dynamic reward weighting for LLM alignment to improve training efficiency and obtain superior Pareto optimal performance across diverse objectives. |

| GAPrune: Gradient-Alignment Pruning for Domain-Aware Embeddings (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10844) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.10844))| Yixuan Tang, yixuantt | GAPrune is a novel pruning framework for domain-aware embedding models, balancing domain-specific importance with general linguistic foundations for efficient compression. The primary objective is to address suboptimal pruning decisions in domain-specific contexts by integrating parameter importance with general linguistic preservation. GAPrune's methodology involves characterizing each parameter using Fisher Information for importance estimation and cross-domain gradient alignment via cosine similarity, combining these into a Domain Alignment Importance (DAI) score to guide pruning. Quantitatively, GAPrune achieves +4.51% improvement on FinMTEB and +1.73% on ChemTEB after 100 steps of retraining at 50% sparsity, outperforming all baselines and maintaining performance within 2.5% of dense models in one-shot pruning. For AI practitioners, GAPrune provides a principled compression strategy that enhances domain-specific capabilities while achieving efficiency, offering a new approach for developing performant and resource-efficient domain-aware AI applications. |
