| Parallel-R1: Towards Parallel Thinking via Reinforcement Learning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07980) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.07980))| Xinyu Yang, Xiaoyang Wang, Wenhao Yu, Hongming Zhang, Tong Zheng | Parallel-R1 introduces the first reinforcement learning (RL) framework for instilling adaptive parallel thinking in LLMs for complex mathematical reasoning tasks. The paper addresses the challenge of activating parallel thinking in LLMs through training, aiming to overcome limitations of supervised fine-tuning by enabling exploration and generalization on real-world problems. Parallel-R1 employs a progressive curriculum, starting with supervised fine-tuning on easier tasks to teach the parallel thinking format, then transitioning to RL with an alternating accuracy and parallel reward strategy for harder problems. The framework achieves an 8.4% average accuracy improvement over sequential thinking RL models. Most significantly, parallel thinking is validated as a mid-training exploration scaffold, unlocking a 42.9% accuracy improvement over the AIME25 baseline after RL. AI practitioners can leverage this progressive RL curriculum and alternating reward design to effectively train LLMs for complex reasoning, enabling adaptive parallel thinking and improving generalization. |
| Mini-o3: Scaling Up Reasoning Patterns and Interaction Turns for Visual

The paper addresses the limitation of existing open-source Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in performing deep, multi-turn reasoning and trial-and-error exploration for challenging visual search tasks. Mini-03 employs a three-pronged approach: constructing the Visual Probe Dataset, developing an iterative data collection pipeline for cold-start supervised fine-tuning, and introducing an over-turn masking strategy in reinforcement learning to prevent penalization of long trajectories. Mini-03 achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging visual search benchmarks, for example, 48.0% accuracy on VisualProbe-Hard compared to DeepEyes' 35.1%. This work offers practical guidance for developing multimodal models with multi-turn interactions, demonstrating that an over-turn masking strategy can enable test-time scaling of interaction depth even with limited training turn budgets, significantly improving performance on complex visual search. |
| Visual Representation Alignment for Multimodal Large Language Models (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07979) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.07979))| Heeseong Shin, Hyungyu Choi, Junwan Kim, Jaewoo Jung, Heeji Yoon | This paper introduces VIsual Representation ALignment (VIRAL), a regularization strategy that aligns a Multimodal Large Language Model's (MLLM) internal visual representations with features from a pre-trained Vision Foundation Model (VFM) to preserve fine-grained visual details. The research investigates whether the standard text-only supervision paradigm in MLLM training causes a loss of visual information and seeks to develop a method to prevent this degradation. VIRAL introduces an auxiliary cosine similarity-based loss during instruction tuning to align the MLLM's intermediate-layer visual features with features from a frozen VFM like DINOv2. The primary results demonstrate consistent improvements, with VIRAL enhancing the LLaVA-1.5 (SigLIPv2) model's performance on the CV-Bench²D benchmark from 58.90% to 62.66% and on MME from 1738.96 to 1835.62. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that incorporating this simple alignment loss during training is an effective and low-overhead method to substantially improve an MLLM's performance on vision-centric tasks requiring detailed spatial reasoning and object grounding. |
| Reconstruction Alignment Improves Unified Multimodal Models (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07295) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.07295))| XuDong Wang, Luke Zettlemoyer, Trevor Darrell, Ji Xie | Reconstruction Alignment (RecA) is an efficient post-training method that enhances the generation and editing fidelity of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) by leveraging dense visual understanding embeddings as supervision. The paper addresses the limitation of conventional UMM training, which relies on sparse image-text captions, by introducing a self-supervised strategy. RecA conditions a UMM on its own visual understanding encoder embeddings and optimizes it to reconstruct the input image with a reconstruction loss, realigning understanding and generation without requiring additional captions. This method substantially improves image generation performance on GenEval from 0.73 to 0.90 and DPGBench from 80.93 to 88.15, achieved with only 27 A100 GPU-hours. RecA offers AI practitioners a resource-efficient and general post-training alignment strategy to significantly boost UMM generation and editing capabilities across diverse architectures using only unlabeled images. |
| UMO: Scaling Multi-Identity Consistency for Image Customization via

The primary objective is to address the challenge of preserving consistent identity and alleviating identity confusion across multiple reference images in generative AI models, which existing one-to-one mapping paradigms struggle with. UMO introduces a novel multi-to-multi matching paradigm that reformulates multi-identity generation as a global assignment optimization problem, operationalized through a Reference Reward Feedback Learning (ReReFL) framework applied to diffusion models. Experimental results demonstrate that UMO significantly boosts identity similarity and mitigates confusion; for example, on the Multi-Subject task from XVerseBench, UMO (Ours) achieved an ID-Sim of 69.09 and an ID-Conf of 78.06, substantially outperforming the UNO baseline's 31.82 ID-Sim and 61.06 ID-Conf. This framework provides AI practitioners with a robust and scalable method to enhance identity fidelity in multi-person image generation applications, setting a new state-of-the-art among open-source methods in identity preservation. |
| Curia: A Multi-Modal Foundation Model for Radiology (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06830) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.06830))| Elodie Ferreres, Helene Philippe, Antoine Saporta, Julien Khlaut, Corentin Dancette | Curia is a multi-modal foundation model for radiology, trained on a large real-world cross-sectional imaging dataset, demonstrating superior performance and emergent cross-modal generalization across 19 diverse tasks. The main objective was to address the impracticality of narrow, single-task AI models in radiology by developing a broadly generalizable foundation model for comprehensive interpretation. Curia was developed by pre-training Vision Transformer models (ViT-B and ViT-L) using the DINOv2 self-supervised learning algorithm on over 200 million CT and MRI images (130 TB from 150K exams), then evaluated by training lightweight prediction heads on frozen features. On the CuriaBench, Curia-L achieved 98.40% accuracy in CT organ recognition, surpassing other FMs, and demonstrated strong cross-modal generalization with a 9.17 percentage point balanced accuracy decrease on CT-to-MRI organ recognition. This offers AI practitioners a robust, data-efficient foundation model, released on Hugging Face, to accelerate the development of versatile diagnostic tools and research, especially in low-data and cross-modal scenarios. |
| F1: A Vision-Language-Action Model Bridging Understanding and Generation

The main objective is to move beyond reactive imitation to robust, foresight-driven robotic policies capable of handling dynamic visual environments and long-horizon tasks. F1 employs a Mixture-of-Transformer architecture with dedicated experts for understanding, visual foresight generation, and action execution, using a next-scale prediction mechanism and a three-stage progressive training recipe. On real-world tasks, F1 achieved an average success rate of 82.2%, notably outperforming the best-performing baseline (πo at 40.0%) on the challenging Handover (R2H) task with a 73.3% success rate. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that coupling predictive foresight with multimodal grounding via a modular, progressively trained VLA framework significantly enhances robustness, generalization, and long-horizon coherence in embodied AI agents, offering a path beyond purely reactive systems. |
| Staying in the Sweet Spot: Responsive Reasoning Evolution via
  Capability-Adaptive Hint Scaffolding (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06923) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.06923))| Yongcheng Zeng, Erxue Min, Zexu Sun, zhaojinm, ChillingDream | SEELE is a novel reinforcement learning framework that leverages capability-adaptive hint scaffolding to optimize LLM reasoning evolution. The primary objective is to identify and dynamically adjust problem difficulty under off-policy guidance to match the model's evolving capabilities, ensuring high learning efficiency. This is achieved through a multi-round rollout sampling strategy and an Item Response Theory (IRT) model, which predict and adaptively control hint length to maintain a theoretical optimal rollout accuracy of approximately 50%. Experimental results demonstrate that SEELE outperforms Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) by +11.8 points and Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) by +10.5 points on average across six math reasoning benchmarks. AI practitioners can utilize this approach to significantly enhance LLM exploration efficiency and reasoning performance by dynamically aligning problem difficulty with model capability throughout the training process. |
| Language Self-Play For Data-Free Training (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07414) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.07414))| Vijai Mohan, Yuandong Tian, Qi Ma, Mengting Gu, Jakub Grudzien Kuba | This paper introduces Language Self-Play (LSP), a reinforcement learning framework enabling large language models to improve through self-generated data in a competitive game without external training data. The main objective is to overcome the data dependency bottleneck in large language model (LLM) training by developing a method for models to improve autonomously without additional data. LSP frames LLM training as a competitive game where a single model acts as both a Challenger (generating difficult queries) and a Solver (responding to them), using Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with KL-divergence regularization and a quality self-reward mechanism to prevent degeneration. Experiments on the AlpacaEval benchmark with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct show that LSP, without external training data, achieved an overall win-rate of 40.6% against the base model, comparable to a data-driven GRPO baseline (40.9%), and further increased the overall win-rate from 40.9% to 43.1% when applied after data-based RL training. AI practitioners can utilize LSP to continuously enhance LLM capabilities without the need for new human-curated datasets, reducing data acquisition and annotation costs and enabling more autonomous model development cycles. |

The primary objective is to address the limitations of standard causal attention in capturing global context and improve token efficiency during large language model pretraining. This is achieved through a hybrid design of causal and lookahead keys, with a mathematical equivalence derived to enable efficient parallel training. Experimental results demonstrate that CASTLE consistently outperforms standard causal attention, with CASTLE-XL achieving a 0.0348 lower validation loss compared to Baseline-XL after 50B training tokens. This indicates that AI practitioners can leverage CASTLE to develop more token-efficient and higher-performing LLMs for pretraining and downstream tasks. |
| Directly Aligning the Full Diffusion Trajectory with Fine-Grained Human
  Preference (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06942) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.06942))| Yingfang Zhang, Shiyi Zhang, Zhantao Yang, Zhimin Li, Xiangwei Shen | This paper introduces Direct-Align and Semantic Relative Preference Optimization (SRPO) to align diffusion models with human preferences across the full denoising trajectory, improving realism and efficiency. The research addresses the challenges of computationally expensive multistep denoising with gradient computation and the reliance on continuous offline reward model adaptation for achieving desired aesthetic quality. The proposed methodology includes Direct-Align for single-step image recovery via a predefined noise prior, enabling full trajectory optimization, and SRPO, which formulates rewards as text-conditioned signals for online adjustment. By fine-tuning the FLUX.1.dev model, SRPO achieves a human-evaluated realism and aesthetic quality improvement of over 3x and demonstrates over 75x greater training efficiency compared to DanceGRPO. For AI practitioners, this method offers a more efficient and robust online reinforcement learning framework for text-to-image generation, significantly enhancing realism and fine-grained aesthetic control in diffusion models with reduced need for extensive offline reward model tuning. |
| SimpleQA Verified: A Reliable Factuality Benchmark to Measure Parametric
  Knowledge (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07968) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.07968))| Dipanjan Das, Sasha Goldshtein, Giovanni D'Antonio, Gal Yona, Lukas Haas | SimpleQA Verified is a new, rigorously curated benchmark designed to reliably evaluate Large Language Model (LLM) parametric factuality. The paper aims to create a more reliable and robust benchmark for short-form LLM factuality, SimpleQA Verified, by addressing critical limitations like noisy labels, topical biases, and question redundancy present in OpenAI's SimpleQA. Its creation involved a multi-stage filtering process including semantic and TF-IDF de-duplication, re-balancing of topic and answer-type distributions, source reconciliation, and adversarial selection of challenging samples, alongside enhancements to the autorater prompt. This benchmark provides AI practitioners with a higher-fidelity tool to accurately measure and track genuine progress in LLM factual recall, helping to mitigate hallucinations and avoid overfitting to benchmark artifacts. |
| Q-Sched: Pushing the Boundaries of Few-Step Diffusion Models with
  Quantization-Aware Scheduling (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01624) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.01624))| Diana Marculescu, Natalia Frumkin | Q-Sched is a novel post-training quantization-aware scheduler for few-step diffusion models that modifies the noise schedule. The main objective is to enable efficient quantization of few-step diffusion models, addressing the computational cost and memory requirements that limit their deployment on resource-constrained hardware. Q-Sched achieves this by optimizing learnable scalar preconditioning coefficients for the sampling trajectory, utilizing a novel reference-free Joint Alignment-Quality (JAQ) loss function. The primary results show Q-Sched achieving full-precision accuracy with a 4x model size reduction and a 15.5% FID improvement over the FP16 4-step Latent Consistency Model. This approach allows AI practitioners to deploy high-fidelity few-step diffusion models on resource-constrained hardware by enabling substantial model compression without compromising generation quality. |
| ΔL Normalization: Rethink Loss Aggregation in RLVR (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07558) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.07558))| Lili Qiu, Yuqing Yang, Yike Zhang, Xufang Luo, Zhiyuan He | ΔL Normalization is a novel loss aggregation method for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) designed to address challenges from dynamic generation lengths. The primary objective is to develop an unbiased, minimum-variance estimator for the true policy gradient, mitigating high gradient variance and unstable optimization in RLVR. The methodology involves reformulating the problem as finding a minimum-variance unbiased estimator, where sample-level gradients are optimally combined by scaling them with response length powers (L_i^a). Experiments demonstrate that ΔL Normalization consistently achieves superior results; for instance, on the CountDown 3B task, it attained an Avg@8 score of 0.847, outperforming GRPO Norm (0.811). This method enables AI practitioners to achieve more stable training dynamics and superior model performance in RLVR systems, particularly those with variable response lengths, with minimal implementation complexity. |
