| Hala Technical Report: Building Arabic-Centric Instruction & Translation
  Models at Scale (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14008) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.14008))| Bernard Ghanem, Mohammad Zbeeb, Hasan Abed Al Kader Hammoud | HALA introduces a family of Arabic-centric instruction and translation models built using an efficient translate-and-tune pipeline. The primary objective was to overcome the scarcity of high-quality Arabic instruction data and build scalable, culturally aligned Arabic LLMs. The methodology involves compressing a strong AR↔EN teacher to FP8 for efficient inference, bootstrapping a 1.25M bilingual corpus, and translating English instruction datasets into Arabic to create a 4.5M instruction corpus. Resulting models (350M, 700M, 1.2B, 9B) are fine-tuned and then merged with their bases using spherical linear interpolation (slerp). HALA models achieved state-of-the-art results on Arabic-centric benchmarks, with HALA-1.2B improving 5.1% in average score over its LiquidAI/LFM2-1.2B base, and the FP8 compression yielding ~2x throughput. This work demonstrates a practical and effective pipeline for developing high-performing, language-centric LLMs for underrepresented languages under constrained compute, emphasizing efficient translation and strategic model merging. |
| SAIL-VL2 Technical Report (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14033) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.14033))| Zijian Kang, Yue Liao, Fangxun Shu, Yongjie Ye, Weijie Yin | This paper introduces SAIL-VL2, an open-suite vision-language foundation model that achieves state-of-the-art performance at 2B and 8B parameter scales. The primary objective is to develop a powerful yet computationally efficient LVM for comprehensive multimodal understanding and reasoning. Its methodology combines a large-scale data curation pipeline with scoring and filtering, a progressive training framework using a specialized SAIL-ViT encoder and an SFT-RL hybrid paradigm, and sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures. The model demonstrates superior performance across 106 datasets; SAIL-VL2-2B ranks first on the OpenCompass leaderboard among open-source models under 4B, and the SAIL-VL2-8B model achieves a score of 95.28 on DocVQA. For AI practitioners, this work provides a blueprint for building high-performance, efficient LVMs at smaller scales, offering an accessible foundation for multimodal applications without requiring massive parameter counts. |
| PANORAMA: The Rise of Omnidirectional Vision in the Embodied AI Era (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12989) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.12989))| Zihao Dongfang, Kaiyu Lei, Ziqiao Weng, Chenfei Liao, Xu Zheng | This paper surveys the field of omnidirectional vision for embodied AI, identifying key challenges in data, models, and applications, and proposes a system architecture called PANORAMA. The paper's objective is to synthesize recent advancements in 360° vision, define the core problem space—data bottlenecks, model capability gaps, and application blanks—and propose a structured roadmap for developing general-purpose omnidirectional AI systems. The methodology is a comprehensive literature review and conceptual synthesis, culminating in the proposed PANORAMA system, a four-subsystem architecture comprising Data Acquisition & Pre-processing, Perception, Application, and Acceleration & Employment. As a survey, the paper presents no novel experimental results but consolidates existing work, identifying 23 representative datasets, including the OSR-Bench dataset for spatial reasoning which contains over 153,000 question-answer pairs. The principal implication for AI practitioners is the critical need to develop specialized, projection-agnostic architectures and large-scale, multi-modal panoramic datasets, as outlined in the proposed system, rather than simply adapting traditional pinhole-vision models for embodied AI applications. |
| GenExam: A Multidisciplinary Text-to-Image Exam (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14232) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.14232))| Yu Qiao, Changyao Tian, Xiangyu Zhao, Penghao Yin, Zhaokai Wang | The paper introduces GenExam, a benchmark that frames text-to-image generation as a multidisciplinary academic exam to evaluate models on complex, knowledge-intensive drawing tasks. The primary objective is to assess the ability of text-to-image models to integrate deep understanding, reasoning, and generation capabilities to satisfy the strict constraints found in exam-style prompts. The methodology involves a curated dataset of 1,000 prompts across 10 subjects, each with a ground-truth image and fine-grained scoring points; an MLLM-as-a-judge protocol evaluates semantic correctness and visual plausibility. The primary result is that even state-of-the-art models struggle significantly, with the top model, GPT-Image-1, achieving a strict score of only 12.1%, while most models scored near 0%. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that GenExam provides a rigorous benchmark for developing and testing generative models that require deep domain knowledge and precise reasoning, highlighting a critical gap between current illustration-focused capabilities and the demands of expert-level, factually accurate image generation. |
| Scrub It Out! Erasing Sensitive Memorization in Code Language Models via
  Machine Unlearning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13755) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.13755))| Zhou Yang, Di Wang, Zhikun Zhang, Yao Wan, Zhaoyang Chu | This paper introduces `CODEERASER`, a machine unlearning method to selectively erase memorized sensitive information from code language models without full retraining. The research investigates whether sensitive information memorized by deployed Code Language Models (CLMs) can be erased effectively and efficiently. The proposed `CODEERASER` method uses a selective gradient-based approach, applying gradient ascent on sensitive code segments to induce forgetting while using gradient descent and a KL divergence constraint on non-sensitive context to maintain model utility. Experiments on the Qwen2.5-Coder-7B model show `CODEERASER` reduces targeted memorization by 93.89% while retaining 99.99% of the model's original code generation performance on the HumanEval benchmark. For AI practitioners, this provides a computationally efficient, post-hoc technique to address privacy vulnerabilities and fulfill data deletion requests for deployed CLMs without the prohibitive cost of complete model retraining. |
| THOR: Tool-Integrated Hierarchical Optimization via RL for Mathematical
  Reasoning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13761) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.13761))| Yicheng Pan, Jiefeng Ma, Pengfei Hu, Zhenrong Zhang, Qikai Chang | This paper introduces THOR, a tool-integrated hierarchical optimization framework via reinforcement learning to enhance the mathematical reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). The primary objective is to overcome challenges in constructing tool-integrated data, performing fine-grained optimization, and enhancing inference for mathematical problem-solving. The methodology combines TIRGen, an actor-critic pipeline for generating policy-aligned tool-use data, with a hierarchical reinforcement learning strategy that jointly optimizes for trajectory-level answer correctness and step-level code generation success. The THOR-Thinking-8B model achieves a state-of-the-art average score of 79.8% across six mathematical benchmarks, including 77.5% on AIME 2024 and 96.8% on MATH 500. For AI practitioners, this paper provides a concrete framework for improving LLM precision on reasoning tasks by integrating external tools, demonstrating that a hierarchical RL approach leveraging intermediate tool feedback is an effective strategy for fine-tuning models. |
| Wan-Animate: Unified Character Animation and Replacement with Holistic
  Replication (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14055) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.14055))| Mingyang Huang, Siqi Hu, Li Hu, Xin Gao, Gang Cheng | Wan-Animate is a unified, open-source framework for high-fidelity character animation and in-video replacement using a reference video for motion and expression. The primary objective is to create a single, holistic model that unifies control over body motion, facial expression, and environmental interaction for both animating a static image and replacing a character within an existing video. The methodology is built upon the `Wan-I2V` video foundation model, utilizing a modified input paradigm with 2D skeleton signals for body control and implicit facial features injected via cross-attention for expression control, supplemented by an auxiliary `Relighting LoRA` for environmental integration. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance against open-source methods, attaining a Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 94.65 on portrait animation tasks, and human evaluations show a clear preference over leading closed-source models. For AI practitioners, this work provides a powerful, open-source model and architectural blueprint for adapting large video foundation models to complex, multi-modal character generation tasks, significantly lowering the barrier to creating high-quality animated content for applications in media and advertising. |
| Improving Context Fidelity via Native Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13683) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.13683))| Xiangru Tang, Shiqi Li, Xinyu Wang, Jinlin Wang, Suyuchen Wang | The paper introduces CARE, a native retrieval-augmented reasoning framework designed to improve the context fidelity of Large Language Models (LLMs). The primary objective is to mitigate context hallucination by training LLMs to dynamically identify, retrieve, and explicitly integrate evidence from provided context directly into their reasoning chains. The methodology involves a two-phase training process: an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase to teach the evidence-integrated reasoning format, followed by a reinforcement learning (RL) phase using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a curriculum learning strategy to refine the native retrieval mechanism. CARE consistently outperforms baselines across multiple QA benchmarks; for instance, with LLaMA-3.1 8B, it achieves a +15.29% average F1 improvement over the original model on real-world QA datasets. For AI practitioners, this work presents a framework to build more reliable LLMs for knowledge-intensive tasks by enhancing their ability to ground answers in provided context, reducing reliance on external retrieval modules. |
| SteeringControl: Holistic Evaluation of Alignment Steering in LLMs (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13450) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.13450))| Zhun Wang, Nathan W. Henry, David Park, Nicholas Crispino, Vincent Siu | This paper introduces STEERINGCONTROL, a benchmark and modular framework for holistically evaluating the effectiveness and behavioral entanglement of representation steering methods in large language models. The primary objective is to systematically assess how well popular steering methods control specific alignment behaviors (e.g., bias, harmfulness, hallucination) and to quantify their unintended side effects on secondary behaviors. The authors created a suite of 17 datasets to distinguish primary (steered) and secondary behaviors, using a standardized framework to test five steering methods (DIM, ACE, CAA, PCA, LAT) on Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B models. Results demonstrate a strong dependency on the specific model-method-behavior combination, revealing significant trade-offs between effectiveness and entanglement; for example, steering Qwen-2.5-7B towards harmful generation using the DIM method increased jailbreaking effectiveness by 72.7% but also induced a 55.5% degradation in sycophancy performance. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that representation steering is not an orthogonal intervention, and applying these methods requires comprehensive, multi-behavioral evaluation to manage the critical effectiveness-entanglement tradeoff before deployment. |
| MARS2 2025 Challenge on Multimodal Reasoning: Datasets, Methods,
  Results, Discussion, and Outlook (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14142) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.14142))| Bowen Zhou, Yaxiong Chen, Jiajun Zhang, Shengwu Xiong, Peng Xu | This paper presents an overview of the MARS2 2025 Challenge on Multimodal Reasoning, detailing the datasets, methods, and results for advancing multimodal large language models (MLLMs). The main objective was to evaluate and advance multimodal reasoning in MLLMs by focusing on real-world, specialized scenarios that require synergistic effects across tasks and non-stepwise complex reasoning. The challenge introduced two new datasets, Lens and AdsQA, and established three competition tracks: Visual Grounding in Real-world Scenarios (VG-RS), Visual Question Answering with Spatial Awareness (VQA-SA), and Visual Reasoning in Creative Advertisement Videos (VR-Ads). The results demonstrated the difficulty of these tasks for current models, with the winning method in the VG-RS track achieving a 66.70% accuracy, highlighting significant performance gaps compared to human-level understanding. For AI practitioners, this research provides validated, challenging benchmarks and a comprehensive analysis showing that successful methods often employ multi-stage alignment strategies combining supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) on domain-specific synthesized data to improve model performance on complex reasoning tasks. |
