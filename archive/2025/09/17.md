| WebWeaver: Structuring Web-Scale Evidence with Dynamic Outlines for
  Open-Ended Deep Research (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13312) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.13312))| Houquan Zhou, Shen Huang, Bo Zhang, Xin Guan, Zijian Li | WebWeaver is a dual-agent framework for open-ended deep research that dynamically structures web-scale evidence to generate high-quality, source-grounded reports. The primary objective is to overcome the limitations of static pipelines and one-shot generation in AI research agents by emulating the human iterative process of concurrent research and planning. The methodology employs a Planner agent that iteratively interleaves evidence acquisition with outline optimization to build a comprehensive plan, and a Writer agent that performs hierarchical, section-by-section synthesis using targeted evidence retrieval from a memory bank. WebWeaver establishes state-of-the-art performance across OEDR benchmarks, achieving a citation accuracy of 93.37% on DeepResearch Bench. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that for complex, information-intensive tasks, agent architectures should separate adaptive planning from hierarchical content synthesis to mitigate long-context failures and improve output reliability. |

| ReSum: Unlocking Long-Horizon Search Intelligence via Context
  Summarization (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13313) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.13313))| Litu Ou, Liwen Zhang, Yida Zhao, Kuan Li, Xixi Wu | This paper introduces ReSum, a paradigm that enables long-horizon reasoning for LLM agents by periodically invoking a specialized tool to summarize conversation history, thus overcoming fixed context window limitations. The main objective is to develop a context management strategy that allows agents to conduct indefinite exploration for complex search tasks that would otherwise exceed their context budget. The methodology involves the ReSum paradigm, which compresses and resets the conversation history using a summary when a token limit is reached, and ReSum-GRPO, a reinforcement learning algorithm that adapts agents to reason from these summarized states by segmenting trajectories for training. The resulting ReSum-GRPO trained WebSailor-30B agent achieves 33.3% Pass@1 on the BrowseComp-zh benchmark, substantially outperforming standard agent paradigms. For AI practitioners, ReSum offers a lightweight method to significantly extend the operational range of existing ReAct-based agents on complex, multi-step tasks by integrating a summarization loop, which can be further optimized with the provided RL adaptation algorithm. |

The main objective is to overcome the inefficiencies of group-based methods like GRPO, such as computational waste from degenerate groups and synchronization bottlenecks that hinder scalability in distributed training. The key methodology involves using a KL-adaptive Bayesian value tracker to maintain a persistent estimate of success probability for each prompt, normalizing advantages globally across the batch, and employing a prioritized sampling curriculum to focus on informative prompts. In experiments with Qwen3-8B, SPO improved the average maj@32 score by +3.4 percentage points over GRPO across five math benchmarks and simulations demonstrated a potential 4.35x training throughput speedup in agentic scenarios. For AI practitioners, SPO provides a more scalable and efficient framework for training LLMs on complex reasoning or agentic tasks, as its group-free design eliminates synchronization barriers and reduces wasted computation, making it better suited for distributed systems with variable-length generation. |

The key methodology is a two-stage pipeline, EconRL, which first employs Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for dynamic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) switching, and then uses Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to train multiple specialized, difficulty-aware reasoning heads via trainable prefixes to enhance parallel sampling efficiency. | Exact Coset Sampling for Quantum Lattice Algorithms (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12341) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.12341))| Yifan Zhang | This paper presents a "pair-shift difference" construction as a correct, assumption-light replacement for a flawed step in a recent windowed-QFT quantum lattice algorithm. The objective is to fix the contested Step 9 from [Chen, 2024], which suffers from a periodicity/support mismatch, and correctly generate a random vector `u` satisfying the modular linear relation `(b*, u) ≡ 0 (mod P)`. The methodology involves creating a coherent copy of the state, shifting it by a uniform variable `T ∈ Z_P` along a known direction `b*`, and subtracting the two states to cancel unknown offset vectors, thereby producing a pure, uniform superposition over a CRT-coset. The primary result is that this procedure produces an output distribution for `u` that is exactly and uniformly supported on the solution space, containing precisely `M₂ⁿ / P` vectors, without relying on amplitude periodicity and with a gate cost of `poly(log M₂)`. For AI practitioners developing quantum algorithms for lattice-based problems, this paper provides a robust subroutine that corrects a critical error in a prior algorithm, ensuring the exactness of the output by eliminating unknown additive offsets which is a common challenge in QFT-based pipelines. |

| Optimal Brain Restoration for Joint Quantization and Sparsification of
  LLMs (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11177) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.11177))| Luca Benini, Yawei Li, Hang Guo | The paper introduces Optimal Brain Restoration (OBR), a novel training-free framework for jointly quantizing and sparsifying Large Language Models (LLMs). OBR's main objective is to reconcile the conflicting demands of quantization and sparsification to enable aggressive LLM compression with minimal performance degradation. It achieves this by formulating a second-order Hessian objective, approximated via row-wise decoupling, and employing group error compensation to redistribute compression-induced errors through a closed-form solution. Experiments demonstrate OBR enables W4A4KV4 quantization with 50% sparsity, achieving up to 4.72x speedup and 6.4x memory reduction compared to FP16-dense baselines, and a perplexity of 8.40 for Llama2-7B compared to 12.94 for SparseGPT+GPTQ under 4-4-4 50% sparsity. This allows AI practitioners to deploy highly efficient, low-bit and sparse LLMs on resource-constrained hardware without retraining, significantly enhancing inference efficiency and reducing memory footprint. |
