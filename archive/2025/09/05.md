| From Editor to Dense Geometry Estimator (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04338) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04338))| Lang Nie, Rongying Liu, Lei Sun, Chunyu Lin, exander | The paper introduces FE2E, a framework adapting a pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) based image editor for monocular dense geometry estimation. The central objective is to systematically evaluate if image editing models are a more effective foundation for dense prediction tasks than their text-to-image generative counterparts. The key methodology involves reformulating the editor's flow matching loss into a "consistent velocity" objective for deterministic tasks, using logarithmic quantization to resolve precision conflicts with BF16, and leveraging the DiT's global attention for cost-free joint estimation of depth and normals. The resulting FE2E model achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance, including a 35% improvement in AbsRel error on the ETH3D dataset, while being trained on only 71k images. For AI practitioners, this research indicates that fine-tuning pre-trained image editing models is a more stable and data-efficient paradigm for dense computer vision tasks due to their inherent structural priors. |
| Drivel-ology: Challenging LLMs with Interpreting Nonsense with Depth (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03867) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.03867))| Chi-Li Chen, Zi Yan Chang, Chia-Yi Hsiao, Chenghao Xiao, Yang Wang | This paper introduces Drivelology, a linguistic phenomenon of "nonsense with depth," and the DRIVELHUB benchmark to evaluate LLMs' ability to interpret such pragmatically complex text. The research objective is to determine if LLMs can grasp the layered semantics and implicit meanings in Drivelology beyond surface-level coherence. The methodology involves evaluating proprietary and open-source LLMs on the new 1,200-sample multilingual DRIVELHUB dataset across four tasks: Drivelology Detection (binary classification), Tagging (multi-label classification), Implicit Narrative Writing (generation), and Narrative Selection (MCQA). Primary results reveal a significant weakness in subtle reasoning, exemplified by a sharp performance drop in the Hard MCQA task where the best open-source model (qwen3-8b-instruct) scored only 26.78% accuracy. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that statistical fluency does not guarantee cognitive comprehension, highlighting a deep representational gap in LLMs' pragmatic understanding that must be addressed when developing systems for tasks requiring nuanced, non-linear, or culturally-embedded reasoning. |
| Towards a Unified View of Large Language Model Post-Training (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04419) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04419))| Hongyi Liu, Youbang Sun, Yuxin Zuo, Xingtai Lv, iseesaw | This paper introduces a unified policy gradient estimator to frame diverse LLM post-training methods and proposes a new algorithm, Hybrid Post-Training (HPT), that dynamically switches between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). The objective is to unify the theoretical understanding of SFT and RL as instances of a single optimization process and leverage this insight to create a more effective post-training strategy. The key methodology is the HPT algorithm, which uses real-time rollout accuracy on a given problem as a feedback signal to select either an SFT loss for exploitation when performance is low or an RL loss (Dr. GRPO) for exploration when performance is high. The primary result shows that HPT with Qwen2.5-Math-7B achieves a 33.0 score on the AIME 2024 benchmark, a 7-point improvement over the LUFFY baseline's 26.1. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that a dynamic, performance-feedback-driven approach to combining SFT and RL can yield superior model reasoning capabilities compared to static mixing or sequential SFT-then-RL pipelines. |
| Inverse IFEval: Can LLMs Unlearn Stubborn Training Conventions to Follow
  Real Instructions? (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04292) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04292))| Yu Fu, Ruijie Miao, Xinping Lei, Qinyan Zhang, zhangysk | This paper introduces Inverse IFEval, a benchmark designed to assess an LLM's ability to follow counter-intuitive instructions that conflict with conventions learned during supervised fine-tuning. The research objective is to quantify this "Counter-intuitive Ability," measuring how well models overcome training-induced "cognitive inertia" when presented with adversarial instructions. The methodology involves a new benchmark of 1012 Chinese and English questions across eight counter-intuitive categories (e.g., "Code without Comments," "Deliberately Incorrect Answers"), evaluated using an optimized LLM-as-a-Judge framework. Primary results demonstrate that even state-of-the-art models struggle, with instruction-tuned models performing particularly poorly; for example, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct's rank drops from 5th on the standard IFEval benchmark to 15th on Inverse IFEval. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that standard alignment techniques can cause overfitting to specific response formats, and robust instruction-following requires training on more diverse and unconventional instructions to improve generalization for real-world, out-of-distribution user requests. |
| DeepResearch Arena: The First Exam of LLMs' Research Abilities via
  Seminar-Grounded Tasks (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01396) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.01396))| Jiaxuan Lu, Meiqi Tu, Junchi Yu, Chen Yang, haiyuanwan | The paper introduces DeepResearch Arena, a benchmark grounded in academic seminar discourse to evaluate the multi-stage research abilities of LLM agents while mitigating data leakage. Its objective is to faithfully assess deep research capabilities by generating tasks from real-world, dynamic expert interactions, addressing the shortcomings of benchmarks based on static corpora or manual curation. The methodology involves a Multi-Agent Hierarchical Task Generation (MAHTG) system that processes over 200 seminars to create 10,000+ tasks, which are evaluated via a hybrid framework combining factual Keypoint-Aligned Evaluation (KAE) and rubric-based Adaptively-generated Checklist Evaluation (ACE). The evaluation of current models shows significant performance disparities, with the `o4-mini-deepresearch` model achieving a top ACE score of 4.03, while other models exhibited trade-offs between factual precision and reasoning depth. For AI practitioners, DeepResearch Arena provides a more realistic and challenging testbed and a dual-metric evaluation protocol to rigorously benchmark and improve agents designed for complex, open-ended research workflows. |
| Transition Models: Rethinking the Generative Learning Objective (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04394) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04394))| Yangguang Li, Xiangyu Yue, Xiaoyu Yue, Yiyuan Zhang, GoodEnough | Transition Models (TiM) introduce a generative learning objective based on arbitrary-interval state transitions, unifying the capabilities of high-fidelity diffusion models and efficient few-step generators within a single model. The research objective is to overcome the quality-versus-computation trade-off inherent in generative models by creating a framework that scales performance monotonically with the number of sampling steps. The key methodology is the formulation of a "State Transition Identity," a continuous-time dynamics equation that constrains the model to learn the entire solution manifold of the generative process, made scalable for large models by a finite-difference approximation called the Differential Derivation Equation (DDE). The primary result is that an 865M parameter TiM model achieves a state-of-the-art GenEval score of 0.67 with a single function evaluation (1-NFE) and scales to 0.83 at 128-NFE, outperforming billion-parameter models like FLUX.1 (12B) and SD3.5 (8B) across all tested step counts. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is a novel training paradigm that produces a single, highly flexible generative model that excels at both rapid, few-step generation and detailed, multi-step refinement, while the DDE method ensures this approach is compatible with standard distributed training frameworks like FSDP for building foundation models. |
| NER Retriever: Zero-Shot Named Entity Retrieval with Type-Aware
  Embeddings (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04011) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04011))| Oren Glickman, Yoav Goldberg, Uri Katz, Or Shachar | This paper presents NER Retriever, a zero-shot framework for retrieving text segments containing entities of ad-hoc, user-defined types by leveraging type-aware embeddings from internal LLM layers. The primary objective is to enable scalable, schema-free named entity retrieval where entity types are specified at query time. The core methodology extracts value vectors from a mid-layer transformer block (LLaMA 3.1 block 17) for entity mentions and type queries, then refines them into a compact space using a lightweight, contrastive-trained projection network. On the MultiCoNER 2 benchmark, NER Retriever achieves an R-Precision of 0.32, significantly outperforming sentence-level embedding baselines like E5-Mistral (0.09). For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that specific internal LLM representations are superior to final-layer embeddings for fine-grained type discrimination, offering a highly effective and efficient strategy for building specialized entity retrieval systems. |
| Few-step Flow for 3D Generation via Marginal-Data Transport Distillation (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04406) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04406))| Lingxi Xie, Chen Yang, Jiemin Fang, Zanwei Zhou, thewhole | The paper introduces MDT-dist, a novel framework for few-step 3D flow distillation to accelerate 3D generative models. The main objective is to distill pretrained 3D flow models to learn Marginal-Data Transport, thereby reducing the numerous iterative sampling steps typically required for 3D content generation. The methodology proposes two optimizable objectives: Velocity Matching (VM) for stable velocity field alignment between student and teacher, and Velocity Distillation (VD) for probability density distillation. When applied to the TRELLIS framework, MDT-dist reduces sampling steps from 25 to 1-2, achieving 0.68s latency (1 step × 2) and a 9.0× speedup on A800, while preserving high visual and geometric fidelity and significantly outperforming existing consistency models. This enables AI practitioners to achieve significantly faster and more efficient high-fidelity 3D content generation for applications such as embodied intelligence simulation and real-time graphics workflows. |
| Video-MTR: Reinforced Multi-Turn Reasoning for Long Video Understanding (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20478) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.20478))| Lionel Ni, Zheng Ge, Tianshui Chen, Yuan Xie | Video-MTR introduces a reinforced multi-turn reasoning framework for long-form video understanding. The objective is to enable iterative key video segment selection and question comprehension, addressing limitations of static reasoning and external visual-language models (VLMs). Video-MTR, built upon Qwen2.5-VL-7B, employs an end-to-end reinforcement learning strategy with a novel gated bi-level reward system that integrates trajectory-level rewards for answer correctness and turn-level rewards for frame-query relevance. It demonstrates superior performance, achieving 48.4% accuracy on MLVU-Test with 32 frames (ranking second to GPT-40) and delivering accuracy improvements of +6.3% on long VideoMME videos compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B. This framework provides AI practitioners with an end-to-end trainable solution for robust long-form video understanding, enhancing accuracy and efficiency for complex tasks by reducing reliance on external VLMs and complex pipelines. |
| Durian: Dual Reference-guided Portrait Animation with Attribute Transfer (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04434) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04434))| Hanbyul Joo, Byungjun Kim, Hyunsoo Cha | Durian is a zero-shot diffusion-based framework that generates portrait animations by transferring facial attributes from a single reference image to a target portrait image. The main objective is to develop a method for generating temporally coherent portrait animation videos that integrate facial attributes from a reference image onto a different target identity, without requiring explicit triplet training data. The key methodology involves a Dual ReferenceNet architecture with separate encoders for the attribute and portrait images, which injects multi-scale spatial features into a denoising U-Net via a spatial attention mechanism, trained using a self-reconstruction scheme on videos with attribute-aware mask expansion and data augmentation. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming 12 baseline models across all metrics; for example, it attained an L1 distance of 0.0744, significantly better than the strongest baseline's score of 0.1023, and enables zero-shot multi-attribute composition. The principal implication for AI practitioners is the provision of a single-stage, scalable framework for creating dynamic, attribute-aware virtual try-on and content generation applications, where the dual-encoder design serves as an effective pattern for composing features from multiple visual references without task-specific fine-tuning. |
| Drawing2CAD: Sequence-to-Sequence Learning for CAD Generation from
  Vector Drawings (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18733) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2508.18733))| Meie Fang, Changmiao Wang, Shichao Lu, Feiwei Qin, 1nnoh | The paper introduces Drawing2CAD, a sequence-to-sequence Transformer framework that generates parametric 3D CAD models directly from 2D vector engineering drawings. The primary objective is to automate the generation of parametric CAD operation sequences from SVG-formatted drawings, preserving the geometric precision inherent in vector formats to better align with industrial design workflows. The methodology utilizes a Transformer encoder and a novel dual-decoder architecture that decouples the generation of CAD command types and their corresponding parameters, guided by a soft target distribution loss function that tolerates minor parameter variations. On the newly created CAD-VGDrawing dataset, Drawing2CAD achieved a command accuracy of 82.43% and reduced the model invalidity ratio to 20.31% with a four-view input, outperforming a vector-input baseline. For AI practitioners, this work provides a framework for complex generative tasks involving structured command-parameter outputs, demonstrating how a dual-decoder architecture can effectively manage coupled yet distinct prediction targets. |
| Delta Activations: A Representation for Finetuned Large Language Models (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04442) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04442))| Ser-Nam Lim, Mayur Naik, Amish Sethi, OscarXZQ | The paper introduces Delta Activations, a vector embedding for finetuned LLMs based on the shift in their internal activations relative to a base model. The objective is to create a compact, semantically meaningful representation to efficiently compare, cluster, and navigate finetuned LLMs without relying on training data or metadata. The methodology involves computing the aggregated difference in hidden state activations between a finetuned model and its base model when processing a fixed set of generic prompts. The primary result is that Delta Activations effectively cluster models by domain, achieving an average silhouette score of 0.614, significantly outperforming baselines like flattened weights (-0.043) and output sentence embeddings (0.087). The principal implication for AI practitioners is the ability to efficiently discover and select relevant models from large repositories for tasks like model merging by creating few-shot task embeddings, thereby facilitating the reuse of publicly available models. |
| False Sense of Security: Why Probing-based Malicious Input Detection
  Fails to Generalize (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03888) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.03888))| Muhao Chen, Qin Liu, Zeming Wei, Cheng Wang | This paper demonstrates that probing-based classifiers for malicious input detection in LLMs fail to generalize because they learn superficial linguistic patterns instead of semantic harmfulness. The primary objective is to systematically investigate why these classifiers, despite high in-domain accuracy, exhibit poor out-of-distribution (OOD) performance for safety detection. The methodology involves controlled experiments comparing probing classifiers against n-gram models, evaluating performance on semantically sanitized datasets where harmful content is replaced but structure is preserved, and testing on paraphrased versions of these datasets. The primary results show a severe performance collapse on OOD data, with accuracy dropping by 15-99 percentage points, and a similar failure on cleaned datasets, confirming reliance on instructional patterns and trigger words rather than semantic content. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that probing internal LLM representations for safety provides a false sense of security, as these detectors are brittle and fail under minor distributional shifts, necessitating more semantically grounded safety mechanisms. |
