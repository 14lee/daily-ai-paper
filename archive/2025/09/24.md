The methodology involves a decoder-only fine-tuning strategy on the Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model, using a custom-built hybrid dataset of 500,000 synthetic and real-world Arabic document-image pairs, while keeping the vision encoder frozen. | MAPO: Mixed Advantage Policy Optimization (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18849) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.18849))| Xuankun Rong, Jian Liang, Yiyang Fang, Quan Zhang, Wenke Huang | MAPO is a policy optimization method that enhances Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) by dynamically mixing advantage functions based on a sample's trajectory certainty to improve foundation model reasoning. The main objective is to address the "advantage reversion" and "advantage mirror" problems inherent in GRPO, where a fixed advantage function provides suboptimal learning signals for samples with different levels of prediction consistency. The key methodology involves introducing "Trajectory Certainty" (measured by the empirical success rate `p` in a rollout group) to dynamically interpolate between the standard z-score advantage and a proposed "Advantage Percent Deviation" (`(r_i - μ) / μ`). This reweighting ensures that high-certainty samples (very easy or very hard) receive a more stable, mean-relative advantage signal, while uncertain samples retain the variance-sensitive standard advantage. On a suite of math reasoning tasks using the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model, MAPO achieved an overall accuracy of 51.26, outperforming the standard GRPO baseline which scored 49.85. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that MAPO offers a more robust and effective method for fine-tuning foundation models with RL. It can be implemented as a modification to the advantage calculation in existing GRPO pipelines to improve training stability and final model performance on reasoning tasks without introducing new model components or requiring additional hyperparameter tuning for the advantage formulation. |

The objective is to overcome the limitations of pixel-aligned methods, such as multi-view inconsistency and fixed Gaussian density, by proposing a voxel-centric paradigm for more geometrically consistent 3D reconstruction. The methodology involves unprojecting 2D image features into a 3D voxel grid using predicted depth, refining this volumetric representation with a sparse 3D U-Net, and then directly regressing 3D Gaussian parameters from each occupied voxel. For AI practitioners, this research shows that shifting from 2D pixel-based to 3D voxel-based feature aggregation fundamentally resolves multi-view alignment issues and allows for adaptive model density, enabling more robust and higher-fidelity 3D reconstructions from sparse image inputs. |

| What Characterizes Effective Reasoning? Revisiting Length, Review, and
  Structure of CoT (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19284) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.19284))| Anthony Hartshorn, Parag Jain, Cheng Zhang, Julia Kempe, Yunzhen Feng | This paper demonstrates that effective Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is characterized not by length or review behavior, but by structural integrity, specifically a low fraction of failed reasoning steps. The research investigates what lexical and structural properties of CoT traces reliably predict correctness across ten large reasoning models on math and science benchmarks. The authors perform a conditional correlation analysis on CoT `Length`, `Review Ratio`, and a novel structural metric, `Failed-Step Fraction` (FSF), derived from an extracted reasoning graph, followed by causal interventions via test-time reranking and controlled CoT editing. The `Failed-Step Fraction` consistently outpredicts `Length` and `Review Ratio` as an indicator of correctness; test-time selection using FSF improved pass@1 accuracy by up to 10% on AIME-25, and editing CoTs to remove failed branches increased subsequent solution accuracy by 8-14%. For AI practitioners, this implies that structure-aware test-time scaling, such as using FSF for reranking candidate solutions or developing methods to prune failed branches during generation, is a more effective strategy for improving reasoning performance than simply increasing CoT length. |

The research objective was to determine if LLMs mirror negative human societal stereotypes by assessing both dialect naming bias (explicitly stating a writer's dialect) and dialect usage bias (providing dialectal text). The methodology involved two experiments—an association task linking adjectives to speakers and a decision task assigning professions—using a novel parallel corpus of seven German dialects against standard German across ten LLMs. The primary results show that all models exhibit significant negative bias; for example, in the association task for the "uneducated" trait, GPT-5 Mini achieved a dialect usage bias score of 1.0, indicating a perfect stereotypical association. The objective is to alleviate the dual burden on the generative model of simultaneously learning long-range mass transport and semantic injection by explicitly making the probability path's endpoints condition-dependent. The methodology introduces learnable, condition-aware additive shifts to the source and/or target distributions, which shortens the required transport path while a theoretical analysis proves this shift-only constraint avoids the mode collapse that affects unrestricted reparameterization. | HyRF: Hybrid Radiance Fields for Memory-efficient and High-quality Novel
  View Synthesis (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17083) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.17083))| Dan Xu, ZipW | HyRF is a hybrid scene representation combining compact explicit Gaussians with decoupled neural fields to achieve memory-efficient, high-quality novel view synthesis with real-time performance. The objective is to reduce the significant memory overhead of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) without sacrificing rendering quality or speed, specifically by overcoming the limitations of neural field compression in capturing high-frequency scene details. The method decomposes scene representation into a sparse set of explicit Gaussians storing 8 base parameters (position, diffuse color, isotropic scale, opacity) and two separate grid-based neural fields that predict residual geometric properties (anisotropy) and view-dependent color, which are then rendered using a hybrid pipeline with visibility pre-culling and a neurally-rendered background. Experiments demonstrate that HyRF achieves state-of-the-art rendering quality while reducing model size by nearly 20x on the Deep Blending dataset (from 676 MB for 3DGS to 34 MB) while improving PSNR from 29.41 to 30.37 and maintaining comparable real-time rendering speeds (114 FPS vs. 112 FPS). For AI practitioners, this hybrid explicit-implicit architecture enables the deployment of high-fidelity, real-time 3D rendering applications in memory-constrained environments where standard 3DGS models are prohibitively large. |

| GeoSVR: Taming Sparse Voxels for Geometrically Accurate Surface
  Reconstruction (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18090) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.18090))| Jin Zheng, Xiao Bai, Youmin Zhang, Jiawei Zhang, Jiahe Li | GeoSVR is an explicit sparse voxel-based framework designed for geometrically accurate, detailed, and complete 3D surface reconstruction from multi-view images. The main objective is to overcome the representational bottlenecks and initialization dependencies of Gaussian Splatting-based methods by leveraging the under-investigated potential of sparse voxels. The key methodology involves a Voxel-Uncertainty Depth Constraint, which robustly integrates monocular depth cues by weighting their influence based on a proposed voxel-level geometric uncertainty metric, and a Sparse Voxel Surface Regularization that employs Voxel Dropout and voxel-wise penalties to enforce geometric consistency and sharp surface formation. Experiments demonstrate superior performance on standard benchmarks, achieving a state-of-the-art mean Chamfer distance of 0.47 on the DTU dataset. The principal implication for AI practitioners is the introduction of an uncertainty-aware mechanism for integrating imperfect external geometric priors, enabling more robust and accurate 3D reconstruction without degrading high-confidence regions. |
