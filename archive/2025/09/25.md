| Video models are zero-shot learners and reasoners (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20328) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.20328))| rgeirhos, kswersky, nmatares, yuxuanli, ThaddaeusWiedemer | This paper demonstrates that large generative video models like Veo 3 exhibit emergent zero-shot learning and reasoning capabilities, positioning them as potential general-purpose vision foundation models analogous to LLMs. The research investigates whether video models trained with a generative, text/video continuation objective on web-scale data can solve a wide variety of vision tasks without task-specific adaptation. The methodology involves prompting the Veo 3 model with an initial image and a text instruction across 62 qualitative and 7 quantitative tasks and evaluating the generated video outputs. The primary results show that Veo 3 can perform a diverse set of tasks from perception to reasoning; for instance, on class-agnostic instance segmentation, it achieves a zero-shot mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 0.74 (pass@10), comparable to the state-of-the-art image editing model Nano Banana. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that these models are on a trajectory to replace multiple bespoke, task-specific computer vision systems with a single, promptable foundation model, heralding a major paradigm shift in machine vision development. |
| SIM-CoT: Supervised Implicit Chain-of-Thought (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20317) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.20317))| Yuhang Cao, Xiaoyi Dong, Yuhang Zang, LiuXR, Wiselnn | SIM-CoT is a training module that introduces step-level supervision to implicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to stabilize training and improve performance without inference overhead. The primary objective is to resolve the "latent instability issue" in implicit CoT, where increasing the number of reasoning tokens causes training to collapse, thereby closing the performance gap with less efficient explicit CoT methods. The key methodology involves using an auxiliary decoder exclusively during training to align each implicit latent token with its corresponding explicit reasoning step, optimizing a step-level loss alongside the final answer loss; this decoder is discarded at inference. The method significantly boosts baseline implicit CoT performance, improving Coconut by +8.2% on GPT-2 and CODI by +3.0% on LLaMA-3.1 8B on the GSM8k-Aug dataset, and surpasses the explicit CoT baseline on GPT-2 by 2.1% with 2.3x greater token efficiency. For AI practitioners, SIM-CoT offers a practical, plug-and-play technique to develop more token-efficient reasoning models that are both more accurate and stable than previous implicit methods, while also enabling interpretability of latent reasoning steps for model debugging. |

The objective is to evaluate if GRPO can outperform standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for generative speech understanding tasks like Spoken Question Answering (SQA) and Automatic Speech Translation (AST). The methodology involves using GRPO to sample multiple text outputs from the SALLM for a given speech input, calculating a BLEU score for each output against a ground-truth reference, and using this score as a reward signal to update the model's policy. On the LibriSQA benchmark, the GRPO-trained Granite Speech 8B model achieved a BLEU score of 46.40, a 6% relative improvement over the SFT baseline of 42.34. For AI practitioners, this indicates that GRPO with a simple, metric-based reward function can be a more effective alternative to SFT for enhancing the performance of SALLMs on open-ended generative speech tasks without requiring a separate reward model. |

| LLMs4All: A Review on Large Language Models for Research and

The main objective is to survey the integration of cutting-edge LLMs into diverse academic fields—including arts, letters, law, economics, business, science, and engineering—to understand how these models are shaping research and practice, and to identify key challenges and future directions. The methodology is a systematic literature review and synthesis; the authors analyze state-of-the-art LLMs (e.g., GPT-series, Claude 3, DeepSeek-R1), benchmark their performance on tasks such as reasoning and coding, and create a taxonomy of applications within each academic discipline based on a unified computational input-output framework. The primary result is a clear demonstration of task specialization among leading LLMs, showing that no single model dominates across all domains; for instance, DeepSeek R1 achieves a top score of 97.3% on the MATH benchmark, whereas reasoner-tuned models like Claude 3.7 Sonnet excel in advanced logical tasks, achieving 84.8% on GPQA Diamond. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that model selection must be strictly guided by specific task requirements, as general-purpose models are consistently outperformed by specialized or fine-tuned models in tasks requiring deep domain knowledge, complex logical reasoning, or quantitative precision, necessitating rigorous, task-aligned evaluation before deployment. |
| EditVerse: Unifying Image and Video Editing and Generation with

The main objective is to overcome video editing data scarcity and architectural fragmentation by creating a single model that enables knowledge transfer from the data-rich image domain. The key methodology involves representing all modalities (text, image, video) as a single interleaved token sequence, processed by a full self-attention transformer with a four-dimensional Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) for spatial, temporal, and sequential awareness. On the proposed EditVerseBench benchmark, the model achieves a VLM editing quality score of 7.65, outperforming other open-source methods like InsV2V (5.21), and wins 51.7% of pairwise user comparisons against the commercial Runway Aleph model. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that a unified architecture can effectively mitigate domain-specific data scarcity by facilitating cross-modal knowledge transfer, enabling emergent capabilities such as performing video editing tasks that were only present in the image editing training data. |
| PhysCtrl: Generative Physics for Controllable and Physics-Grounded Video

The main objective is to develop a framework for image-to-video generation that overcomes the lack of physical plausibility in existing models by enabling explicit control over physical parameters and external forces. The key methodology involves a diffusion-based generative physics network, trained on a large-scale synthetic dataset of 550K animations, which learns to predict 3D point trajectories conditioned on material properties and forces; this network uses a novel spatiotemporal attention block and is trained with a physics-based loss derived from the Material Point Method (MPM). The primary result is that PhysCtrl generates superior physics-plausible trajectories, achieving a 77.03% volume Intersection over Union (vIoU), significantly outperforming the next best baseline's 53.78%. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that physical dynamics can be decoupled from visual synthesis, allowing a specialized, physics-informed model to generate 3D trajectories that serve as a strong, controllable conditional signal for existing large-scale video generation models to produce more realistic content. |
| Logics-Parsing Technical Report (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19760) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.19760))| Fan Yang, Shuzhao Li, Xiangyang Chen, ZjuCv, xiuwenzhu | This report introduces Logics-Parsing, an end-to-end Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) framework augmented with reinforcement learning for complex document parsing. The main objective is to overcome the limitations of standard LVLMs in handling complex layouts and inferring correct reading order in documents like multi-column newspapers. The methodology employs a two-stage SFT-then-RL training paradigm: first, Supervised Fine-Tuning on over 300K diverse document images, followed by Layout-Centric Reinforcement Learning (LC-RL) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a multi-component reward for text, layout, and order. On the newly created LogicsParsingBench, Logics-Parsing achieves state-of-the-art performance, reducing the reading order edit distance on Chinese documents from 0.155 to 0.113 after the RL stage. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that augmenting a foundational SFT model with a targeted LC-RL stage on hard-mined samples is a highly effective strategy for developing robust document intelligence systems capable of parsing structurally complex, real-world documents. |

The primary objective is to develop a single MDM that unifies high-performance understanding and generation capabilities, overcoming the performance and modality limitations of prior unified models. Its key methodology is an "Elastic Mixture-of-Transformers" (Elastic-MoT) architecture that couples a large understanding branch (8B parameters) with a smaller generation branch (2.4B parameters), and incorporates explicit planning and self-reflection mechanisms to leverage understanding for improving generation. The model demonstrates superior efficiency and performance, achieving a 6.8x inference speedup on object grounding tasks compared to the autoregressive model Qwen2.5-VL-7B while also surpassing its accuracy. For AI practitioners, the Elastic-MoT design provides a parameter-efficient blueprint for extending large, existing understanding-focused models with generative functionalities, offering a scalable path to building unified multimodal systems without training a massive dense model from scratch. |
| On the Use of Agentic Coding: An Empirical Study of Pull Requests on
  GitHub (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14745) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.14745))| Hajimu Iida, Brittany Reid, Yutaro Kashiwa, Miku Watanabe, hao-li | This empirical study analyzes 567 GitHub pull requests generated by the Claude Code agent to assess their characteristics, acceptance, and required revisions compared to human contributions. The study's primary objective is to investigate the practical utility of autonomous AI-generated code contributions by examining how they differ from human-authored pull requests, their acceptance rates, and the reasons for rejection or revision. The authors compared 567 agent-generated pull requests (Agentic-PRs) with a matched set of 567 human-generated pull requests (Human-PRs) from 157 open-source projects, using manual classification for PR purpose and revision type, alongside statistical analysis of acceptance rates and change metrics. The analysis reveals that 83.8% of Agentic-PRs are accepted, with 54.9% merged without any modification; when revisions are required, they most frequently target bug fixes (45.1%), documentation updates (27.4%), and refactoring (25.7%). For AI practitioners, this research indicates that while agentic coding tools are effective for generating initial code for tasks like refactoring and testing, human oversight remains critical to correct functional bugs, synchronize documentation, and ensure adherence to project-specific architectural and style standards before merging. |
