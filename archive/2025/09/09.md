| Reverse-Engineered Reasoning for Open-Ended Generation (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06160) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.06160))| Wangchunshu Zhou, Minghao Liu, Qixin Xu, Haoran Que, Haozhe Wang | This paper introduces Reverse-Engineered Reasoning (REER), a paradigm that computationally synthesizes deep reasoning trajectories for open-ended generation by working backward from high-quality outputs to train a competitive 8B parameter model. The research aims to instill complex reasoning in LLMs for non-verifiable domains like creative writing without relying on reinforcement learning or costly instruction distillation. REER is operationalized as a gradient-free local search that iteratively refines an initial thinking plan to minimize the perplexity of a known good solution, a process used to create the DeepWriting-20K dataset. The resulting DeepWriter-8B model achieves a score of 91.28 on LongBench-Write, outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o (83.1) and Claude 3.5 (89.3). The principal implication for practitioners is that this perplexity-guided synthesis provides a scalable, cost-effective method and a public dataset to train smaller models with sophisticated planning capabilities for creative tasks, reducing dependency on proprietary systems for data generation. |

The methodology involves an LLM first performing model-based exploration to build an information space and create initial QA pairs, followed by an iterative evolution stage that obfuscates and removes salient information to increase query difficulty; this data is then used for supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning on an 8B model. The research objective is to mitigate VLM performance degradation in visually complex environments by leveraging the model's innate attention mechanisms without retraining. The key methodology involves contrasting the attention map from a general instruction (capturing visual noise) with the attention map from a task-specific question (capturing signal plus noise) to derive a purely semantic attention mask for the input image. Experiments show CARVE consistently improves performance, with one significant result being a 71.83% relative accuracy improvement for the LLAVA1.5-7B model on the V* benchmark (from 38.7% to 66.5%). | Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning Models Is Not Effective for
  Knowledge-Intensive Tasks Yet (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06861) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.06861))| See-Kiong Ng, Bryan Hooi, James Xu Zhao | This paper demonstrates that increasing inference-time computation via test-time scaling for large reasoning models does not consistently improve performance on knowledge-intensive tasks. The research objective was to evaluate whether increasing a model's "thinking time" enhances factual accuracy and reduces hallucination rates. The methodology involved evaluating 12 reasoning models on the SimpleQA and FRAMES benchmarks by systematically increasing their test-time computation via parameters like "reasoning effort" and measuring changes in accuracy and hallucination ratio. The primary result is that increased computation often fails to improve accuracy and can increase hallucinations; for instance, on SimpleQA, GPT-5-mini's hallucination ratio increased by over 15% as its reasoning length grew. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that allocating more inference-time computation is not a reliable strategy for improving factual robustness and can be counterproductive, as extended reasoning may induce confirmation bias and lead to overconfident, incorrect outputs. |

| Saturation-Driven Dataset Generation for LLM Mathematical Reasoning in
  the TPTP Ecosystem (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06809) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.06809))| Damien Sileo, Valentin Quesnel | This paper presents a saturation-driven framework to generate a large-scale, guaranteed-valid dataset for LLM mathematical reasoning from the TPTP axiom library. The main objective is to create a scalable pipeline for producing difficulty-controlled symbolic reasoning tasks to diagnose and address the core weaknesses of LLMs in multi-step logical deduction. The key methodology uses the E-prover in saturation mode to generate an exhaustive derivation graph from axioms, filters the resulting theorems for "interest" using AGInTRater, and formulates three tasks (entailment, premise selection, proof reconstruction) validated by the Vampire prover. Zero-shot experiments reveal that performance systematically degrades with logical depth; the largest model's average score dropped from 93% on easy tasks to 44% on very hard tasks, with performance collapsing on the proof reconstruction task. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that this framework provides both a diagnostic tool to pinpoint fundamental weaknesses in structural reasoning and a scalable source of purely symbolic data for fine-tuning models to improve these core, hierarchical reasoning skills that are not overcome by model scale alone. |
