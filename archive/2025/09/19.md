| FlowRL: Matching Reward Distributions for LLM Reasoning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15207) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.15207))| Hengli Li, Dinghuai Zhang, jayyoung0802, daixuancheng, xuekai | FlowRL is a reinforcement learning framework that matches the full reward distribution using a flow-balanced objective to enhance reasoning diversity and performance in large language models. The main objective is to overcome the mode collapse and reduced solution diversity observed in traditional reward-maximizing RL methods (e.g., PPO, GRPO) by aligning the policy's output distribution with a complete target reward distribution. The key methodology involves transforming scalar rewards into a normalized target distribution using a learnable partition function and minimizing the reverse KL divergence between the policy and this target, which is practically implemented as a GFlowNet-inspired trajectory balance objective with length normalization and importance sampling to stabilize training for long sequences. On math benchmarks, FlowRL achieves an average improvement of 10.0% over the GRPO baseline and 5.1% over PPO, and it consistently outperforms these methods on code reasoning tasks. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that adopting reward distribution-matching via flow-balanced objectives, rather than simple reward maximization, offers a more effective approach for fine-tuning LLMs, leading to improved generalization and exploration of diverse, valid reasoning paths. |
| Reasoning over Boundaries: Enhancing Specification Alignment via

The primary objective is to formalize and address the challenge of "specification alignment," where LLMs must adhere to dynamic, scenario-specific safety and behavioral rules, navigating the inherent trade-off between them. The authors developed SPECBENCH, a benchmark with 5 scenarios and 103 specifications, and an evaluation metric called Specification Alignment Rate (SAR). They also proposed ALIGN3, a single-pass, three-step Test-Time Deliberation (TTD) method that uses hierarchical reflection and revision to reason over specification boundaries at inference time. Experiments show that test-time deliberation improves alignment, with the proposed ALIGN3 method advancing the safety-helpfulness trade-off; applying ALIGN3 to the Qwen3-14B model improved its SAR by up to 11.89 percentage points over its vanilla instruct counterpart, achieving a score of 62.92%. AI practitioners can use the SPECBENCH benchmark to evaluate alignment gaps in LLMs for specific applications and employ lightweight, test-time methods like ALIGN3 to enhance model compliance with custom safety and behavioral policies without costly retraining. |
| Evolving Language Models without Labels: Majority Drives Selection,
  Novelty Promotes Variation (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15194) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.15194))| Kishan Panaganti, Wenhao Yu, Haolin Liu, invokerliang, yujunzhou | This paper introduces EVOL-RL, a label-free reinforcement learning framework that prevents entropy collapse during LLM self-improvement by combining majority-vote selection with a novelty-based variation reward. The research objective is to enable LLMs to autonomously evolve on unlabeled data without the performance degradation and diversity loss common in methods that rely solely on self-consistency or majority-vote signals. The key methodology is a novel reward function, optimized with GRPO, that anchors learning to the majority-voted answer (selection) while simultaneously rewarding responses with semantically distinct reasoning paths (variation), supplemented by asymmetric clipping and an entropy regularizer. EVOL-RL significantly outperforms the majority-only TTRL baseline, with one experiment showing that training a Qwen3-4B-Base model on label-free AIME24 data lifted pass@1 accuracy on the AIME25 benchmark from 4.6% to 16.4%. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that explicitly incentivizing semantic diversity is critical for robust, generalizable self-improvement in label-free settings, providing a practical method to continuously train models without external verifiers or ground-truth labels. |
| Understand Before You Generate: Self-Guided Training for Autoregressive
  Image Generation (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15185) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.15185))| Xihui Liu, Wenlong Zhang, Yuqing Wang, GoodEnough, YueXY233 | This research introduces ST-AR, a self-guided training framework that integrates self-supervised learning objectives with next-token prediction to enhance the visual understanding and generation quality of autoregressive models. The work's primary objective is to identify and address the inherent limitations of the next-token prediction paradigm in learning high-level visual semantics for autoregressive image generation, specifically local dependence, semantic inconsistency, and spatial invariance deficiency. The proposed Self-guided Training for AutoRegressive models (ST-AR) framework augments the standard token prediction loss with three self-supervised objectives: a masked image modeling loss applied to attention maps, an inter-step contrastive loss for temporal semantic consistency, and an inter-view contrastive loss for spatial invariance. ST-AR significantly improves model performance; for instance, it achieves an approximate 49% FID improvement for the LlamaGen-XL model on ImageNet while maintaining the original sampling strategy. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that the generative quality of autoregressive models can be substantially enhanced by directly integrating self-supervised learning tasks into the training process, which improves the model's intrinsic semantic understanding without requiring external representation models, architectural changes, or modifications to the inference procedure. |

The methodology involves creating a benchmark with three distinct, analyst-inspired tasks (Time-Sensitive Data Fetching, Simple Historical Lookup, Complex Historical Investigation) annotated by 70 financial experts, spanning global and Greater China markets, and evaluating 21 models using an LLM-as-a-Judge protocol validated with human oversight. The key methodology integrates three components at inference time: Intra-Step Recursive Refinement (IRR) to inject trajectory data at each denoising step, Flow-Gated Latent Fusion (FLF) to selectively update motion-relevant latent channels, and Dual-Path Self-Corrective Guidance (DSG) to mitigate artifacts by dynamically correcting the guided generation path. The framework achieves state-of-the-art results, scoring a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of 96.08 on static 3D scene generation, significantly outperforming the next-best baseline's 111.49. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that this plug-and-play approach allows them to leverage powerful, off-the-shelf video diffusion models for complex 3D/4D tasks with precise control, eliminating the need for costly, task-specific retraining. |

| Mind the Gap: A Closer Look at Tokenization for Multiple-Choice Question
  Answering with LLMs (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15020) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.15020))| Katharina von der Wense, MinhDucBui, mario-sanz | This research demonstrates that tokenizing the leading space character together with the answer letter in multiple-choice question answering (MCQA) significantly improves large language model (LLM) performance and calibration. The study's objective was to quantify the performance difference between two tokenization strategies for the space preceding the answer label: tokenizing the space separately from the letter ("X") versus tokenizing them together ("_X"). The methodology involved evaluating 15 LLMs on MMLU and five other MCQA datasets, measuring accuracy and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) under both tokenization schemes. The primary result is that tokenizing the space together with the answer letter consistently leads to statistically significant improvements, increasing accuracy by as much as 11% and altering model rankings on leaderboards. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that evaluation frameworks for MCQA must standardize on the space-letter tokenization strategy to ensure results are reliable, comparable, and not skewed by this seemingly trivial implementation detail. |

| FSG-Net: Frequency-Spatial Synergistic Gated Network for High-Resolution

The primary objective is to systematically disentangle genuine semantic changes from nuisance radiometric variations while effectively fusing multi-level features for precise segmentation. The key methodology employs a Discrepancy-Aware Wavelet Interaction Module (DAWIM) in the frequency domain to suppress pseudo-changes, a Synergistic Temporal-Spatial Attention Module (STSAM) to enhance genuine change saliency, and a Lightweight Gated Fusion Unit (LGFU) to merge deep semantics with shallow details. FSG-Net achieves state-of-the-art performance, establishing an F1-score of 94.16% on the CDD benchmark. For AI practitioners, this research validates the effectiveness of a hybrid frequency-spatial processing pipeline as an architectural pattern to enhance model robustness against domain shifts and stylistic variations in visual data. |
