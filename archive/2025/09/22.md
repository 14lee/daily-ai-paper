| RPG: A Repository Planning Graph for Unified and Scalable Codebase
  Generation (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16198) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.16198))| Steven Liu, Xin Zhang, Kyleraha, Cipherxzc, Luo2003 | The paper introduces the Repository Planning Graph (RPG), a persistent graph representation that unifies proposal- and implementation-level planning for scalable codebase generation. Its primary objective is to overcome the limitations of natural language in representing complex software structures, enabling coherent and reliable planning for generating complete repositories from scratch. RPG encodes functional goals, file structures, data flows, and function designs, forming the basis for ZeroRepo, a graph-driven framework that constructs the graph through proposal and implementation-level planning, followed by graph-guided code generation and test validation. On the RepoCraft benchmark, ZeroRepo generates repositories averaging nearly 36K LOC, roughly 3.9x larger than the strongest baseline (Claude Code), and achieves an 81.5% functional coverage and 69.7% pass rate. This demonstrates RPG's ability to model complex dependencies and enable near-linear scaling, providing AI practitioners with a robust, structured foundation for large-scale, long-horizon repository development and enhanced agent localization. |
| MANZANO: A Simple and Scalable Unified Multimodal Model with a Hybrid
  Vision Tokenizer (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16197) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.16197))| jialingt, haosoul122, haotiz, bpan, FrozzZen | Manzano is a simple, scalable unified multimodal model that integrates visual understanding and generation using a novel hybrid vision tokenizer. The primary objective is to overcome the performance trade-off in unified multimodal LLMs, which arises from the conflicting requirements of continuous embeddings for understanding and discrete tokens for autoregressive generation. Manzano's methodology employs a single shared vision encoder that feeds both continuous and discrete adapters into a unified autoregressive LLM, with an auxiliary diffusion decoder for pixel rendering, all trained through a multi-stage recipe across diverse data. Key results demonstrate minimal task conflict; the Manzano 3B model shows a +18.8 gain on Knowledge benchmarks compared to the 300M baseline, and the 30B model achieves 96.2 on ScienceQA (understanding) and SOTA among unified models on GenEval (0.85) and WISE (0.54) generation benchmarks. This approach offers AI practitioners a robust framework for developing unified multimodal LLMs that achieve competitive performance in both visual understanding and generation tasks without compromising either capability. |
| Latent Zoning Network: A Unified Principle for Generative Modeling,

The research investigates whether a single framework can address these traditionally distinct ML tasks. LZN's methodology involves encoders mapping diverse data types into unique latent zones and decoders mapping latents back to data, with tasks formulated as compositions of these components and latent alignment. Key quantitative results include LZN improving FID on CIFAR10 from 2.76 to 2.59 for image generation and outperforming MoCo by 9.3% for unsupervised representation learning on ImageNet linear classification. This framework enables AI practitioners to integrate generative, representation learning, and classification capabilities within a single model, potentially streamlining ML development and fostering synergy across tasks. |

The primary objective is to generate realistic and semantically consistent 3D indoor scenes, addressing existing challenges in visual quality, diversity, semantic consistency, and user control, particularly by mitigating the lack of a large-scale, high-quality dataset. The methodology centers on a novel multi-view multi-modal diffusion model, trained on a new dataset of 12,328 structured scenes and 4.7M renderings, which synthesizes appearance, geometry, and semantic maps from arbitrary viewpoints using a layout-guided alternating cross-view and cross-modal attention mechanism, followed by iterative dense view generation and 3D Gaussian splatting reconstruction. Quantitatively, SPATIALGEN with layout guidance achieved a PSNR of 17.59 and LPIPS of 0.32 for image-to-3D scene generation under a forward camera trajectory, demonstrating substantial improvement over the variant without layout guidance (PSNR 11.47, LPIPS 0.59). This provides AI practitioners with a more robust and controllable framework for creating photorealistic and consistent 3D indoor environments, directly benefiting applications in interior design, virtual reality, and robotics by enhancing scene synthesis quality and consistency. |

| A Vision-Language-Action-Critic Model for Robotic Real-World
  Reinforcement Learning (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15937) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.15937))| Jiangmiao, simonlin123, andyzsz123, haoranzhang, fuxian | This paper introduces VLAC, a Vision-Language-Action-Critic model that unifies reward generation and action policy for efficient real-world robotic reinforcement learning. Its main objective is to overcome sparse, handcrafted rewards and inefficient exploration by providing dense progress signals and enabling self-improvement through a single integrated actor-critic model. VLAC, built on InternVL, is trained on diverse vision-language and robot/human trajectory datasets to predict dense progress deltas as rewards and generate actions autoregressively, integrating into an asynchronous real-world RL loop with PPO and human-in-the-loop interventions. Experiments demonstrate VLAC improved success rates from approximately 30% to 90% within 200 real-world episodes, with human-in-the-loop interventions further yielding a 50% improvement in sample efficiency and achieving up to 100% final success. This framework offers AI practitioners a practical recipe for developing generalizable robotic systems by eliminating task-specific reward engineering and enabling data-efficient, real-world online reinforcement learning with human guidance. |
| RGB-Only Supervised Camera Parameter Optimization in Dynamic Scenes (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15123) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.15123))| Narendra Ahuja, Hao Zhang, fangli3 | The paper introduces ROS-Cam, an RGB-only supervised method for accurate and efficient camera parameter optimization in dynamic scenes. The primary objective is to accurately and efficiently estimate camera parameters in dynamic scenes solely from a single RGB video without relying on ground truth priors. ROS-Cam employs patch-wise tracking filters for robust pseudo-supervision, an outlier-aware joint optimization with learnable uncertainty (Cauchy distribution, Average Cumulative Projection error) to handle moving objects, and a two-stage optimization strategy for stability and speed. The method achieves superior performance, notably on MPI-Sintel, where for 'alley_1' it yields an ATE of 0.002, RPE trans of 0.003, and RPE rot of 0.038, outperforming baselines, and demonstrates significant efficiency with mean camera optimization times of 20 minutes on iPhone datasets. AI practitioners in 3D reconstruction and visual odometry can utilize ROS-Cam for highly accurate and efficient camera parameter estimation in dynamic scenes, overcoming the need for expensive and often unavailable ground truth data. |
| Do You Hear What I Mean? Quantifying the Instruction-Perception Gap in
  Instruction-Guided Expressive Text-To-Speech Systems (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13989) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.13989))| Hung-yi Lee, Kuan-Yu Chen, Tzu-Chieh Wei, Huang-Cheng Chou, Yi-Cheng Lin | This paper quantifies the instruction-perception gap in Instruction-Guided Expressive Text-To-Speech (ITTS) systems through human perceptual evaluations. The primary objective was to investigate the relationship between natural-language instructions and listener perception in ITTS, assessing their controllability of fine-grained expressivity and fundamental speech aspects. A comprehensive evaluation framework utilized objective acoustic analysis and subjective human perceptual judgments across four control dimensions: Adverbs of Degree, Emotion-Intensity Adjective, Speaker Age, and Word-level Emphasis. Five leading ITTS models were tested using the E-VOC corpus, comprising over 60,000 human ratings. Results showed that gpt-4o was the only model reliably translating degree modifiers and graded adjectives into perceptually ordered changes in loudness, pitch, speaking rate, and emotion intensity. However, all systems struggled with fine-grained controls; for instance, gpt-4o's highest accuracy for Word-level Emphasis was only 0.265. AI practitioners should note that while contemporary ITTS can handle high-level styles with coarse reliability, achieving consistent and fine-grained control aligned with human perception remains a significant open challenge for future development. |

| WhisTLE: Deeply Supervised, Text-Only Domain Adaptation for Pretrained
  Speech Recognition Transformers (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10452) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.10452))| Karun Kumar, Akshat Pandey, tetrisd | WhisTLE introduces a deeply supervised, text-only domain adaptation technique for pretrained encoder-decoder ASR transformers like Whisper. The objective is to enable effective domain adaptation for pretrained ASR models using only text data, circumventing the need for costly target domain speech collection. WhisTLE's methodology involves training a variational autoencoder (VAE) to map target domain text inputs to internal latent representations generated by the original ASR encoder from speech, subsequently fine-tuning the ASR decoder using this text-to-latent encoder, optionally combined with text-to-speech (TTS) adaptation. This approach achieved an average 12.3% relative Word Error Rate (WER) reduction compared to TTS-only adaptation and outperformed all non-WhisTLE baselines in 27 out of 32 experimental scenarios across various out-of-domain datasets and ASR models. For AI practitioners, WhisTLE offers a practical and effective method for enhancing ASR performance in new domains without requiring new speech data, leveraging deep latent supervision for superior adaptation over input-output supervised methods. |
| Ask-to-Clarify: Resolving Instruction Ambiguity through Multi-turn
  Dialogue (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15061) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.15061))| Hui Zhang, Sicheng Xie, Tianyi Lu, Xinghao Zhu, leolin9248 | The Ask-to-Clarify framework enables embodied agents to resolve ambiguous instructions through multi-turn dialogue and generate end-to-end low-level actions for real-world tasks. Its main objective is to overcome the limitation of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that passively execute instructions, by allowing agents to actively seek clarification from human users when faced with ambiguity. The framework integrates a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for collaborative dialogue and a diffusion model for action generation, linked by a connection module, and is trained using a two-stage knowledge-insulation strategy. In real-world experiments across 8 tasks, Ask-to-Clarify significantly outperformed existing VLAs, achieving a 95.0% average success rate on "Put the Object on the plate" tasks. This demonstrates a clear path toward building truly collaborative and reliable embodied agents capable of effective human-robot interaction in complex real-world settings. |
