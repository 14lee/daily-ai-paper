| Why Language Models Hallucinate (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04664) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04664))| Edwin Zhang, Santosh S. Vempala, Ofir Nachum, Adam Tauman Kalai | This paper argues that language models hallucinate because training objectives and evaluation procedures reward guessing over acknowledging uncertainty. The objective is to analyze the statistical causes of hallucinations by reducing the unsupervised problem of language generation to a supervised binary classification task ("Is-It-Valid"), thereby linking generative errors to misclassification and model calibration. A primary result is a formal lower bound showing the generative error rate is at least twice the binary misclassification rate; for arbitrary facts, the hallucination rate is at least the fraction of training examples appearing only once (the singleton rate). The principal implication for AI practitioners is that mitigating hallucinations requires fundamentally altering the scoring of dominant evaluation benchmarks to stop penalizing uncertainty, as the current binary grading paradigm reinforces hallucinatory behavior. |
| Symbolic Graphics Programming with Large Language Models (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05208) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.05208))| Kaipeng Zhang, Zeju Qiu, Haoquan Zhang, Yamei Chen, YangyiH | This research introduces a benchmark and a reinforcement learning (RL) method using verifiable rewards from vision foundation models to improve the generation of Symbolic Graphics Programs (SGPs) from natural language using LLMs. The primary objective is to assess and enhance the ability of LLMs, particularly open-source models, to perform symbolic graphics programming. The key methodology involves finetuning a base LLM (Qwen-2.5-7B) with RL, where rewards are derived from a format-validity gate and cross-modal alignment scores from vision encoders like SigLIP and DINO. The primary result is a significant performance improvement; the RL-tuned model increased its overall compositional score on the SGP-COMPBENCH from 8.8 to 60.8, substantially narrowing the gap with leading proprietary models. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that RL with cross-modal rewards offers a scalable approach to inject visual knowledge into LLMs, enhancing their ability to generate structured visual code without requiring ground-truth program-image datasets. |
| Set Block Decoding is a Language Model Inference Accelerator (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04185) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04185))| Jeremy Reizenstein, Daniel Haziza, Marton Havasi, Heli Ben-Hamu, Itai Gat | Set Block Decoding (SBD) is a language model inference acceleration method that integrates masked and next-token prediction to sample multiple, non-consecutive future tokens in parallel by fine-tuning existing autoregressive models. The objective is to accelerate the decoding stage of large language models by reducing the number of sequential forward passes required for generation, without sacrificing model performance or requiring architectural changes. The key methodology involves fine-tuning a standard Next Token Prediction (NTP) model with a combined loss function including both NTP and Masked Token Prediction (MATP) objectives, and during inference, generating blocks of future tokens by iteratively unmasking subsets in parallel using solvers like the Entropy Bounded (EB) Sampler. Primary results demonstrate that SBD enables a 3-5x reduction in the number of forward passes while achieving performance equivalent to the original NTP model; for example, a fine-tuned Llama-3.1 8B model achieved a 3.72x speedup on LiveCodeBench-V6 with a Pass@1 score of 31.7, compared to the NTP baseline's 31.5. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that they can achieve significant inference acceleration on existing autoregressive models through a direct fine-tuning process, avoiding the complexity of auxiliary draft models or architectural modifications, thus providing a cost-effective solution for deploying LLMs in latency-sensitive applications. |

The objective is to overcome the prevalent trade-off between reconstruction quality and real-time performance in online 3D reconstruction systems. The key methodology combines a sliding window mechanism, which facilitates direct information exchange between image tokens of adjacent frames, with a compact global camera token pool that stores historical camera information to provide global context for pose estimation. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance, demonstrating superior reconstruction accuracy and pose estimation while maintaining a real-time inference speed of 17.2 FPS. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is the use of a compact camera token pool as a lightweight and efficient global memory mechanism, providing a scalable approach to incorporate historical context in streaming data applications like robotics and AR without the high computational and storage costs of caching full attention keys and values. |
| On Robustness and Reliability of Benchmark-Based Evaluation of LLMs (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04013) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04013))| Kevin Roitero, Stefano Mizzaro, Vincenzo Della Mea, Riccardo Lunardi | This research systematically assesses the robustness of 34 LLMs to linguistic variation by measuring performance on paraphrased questions from standard benchmarks. The study's primary objective is to determine if benchmark-based evaluations are reliable and if LLMs maintain effectiveness when questions are reworded. The methodology involved generating five distinct paraphrases for questions across six benchmarks, including MMLU and HellaSwag, and evaluating model performance in a zero-shot setting. Key findings reveal that while relative model rankings remain highly stable (Kendall's τ > 0.9), absolute accuracy drops significantly, with models providing inconsistent answers to 15%-30% of questions across their paraphrased versions. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that high scores on static benchmarks may overestimate a model's true generalization capabilities, necessitating the adoption of robustness-aware evaluation frameworks that account for real-world linguistic variability. |

The primary objective is to develop a model that concurrently addresses localized disease detection (under-reading), holistic image understanding (inattentional blindness), and consistent reporting by overcoming the limitations of single-scale alignment in existing vision-language models. The key methodology involves a multi-scale alignment loss derived from mutual information maximization, combined with LLM-rewritten reports and a Radiology Semantic Matching Bank (RSMB) to provide robust, semantically-enriched supervision. MedVista3D achieves state-of-the-art performance, with one configuration attaining a global zero-shot disease classification AUC of 0.782 on the CT-RATE dataset, outperforming the prior CT-CLIP baseline by 7.4 percentage points. The principal implication for AI practitioners is that a unified multi-scale alignment objective, enhanced with semantic supervision, provides a robust framework for building powerful foundation models for complex 3D medical imaging that excel at both local- and global-level tasks. |

| Behavioral Fingerprinting of Large Language Models (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04504) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04504))| Xing Li, Zhiyuan Yang, Ying Zhang, Hui-Ling Zhen, Zehua Pei | This paper introduces "Behavioral Fingerprinting," a framework for evaluating the intrinsic cognitive and interactive styles of LLMs beyond standard performance metrics. The research objective is to create multifaceted behavioral profiles that reveal how different models think, distinguishing them on axes like reasoning style, biases, and robustness. The methodology involves probing eighteen LLMs with a curated Diagnostic Prompt Suite and using a powerful judge LLM (Claude-opus-4.1) to automatically score responses against detailed rubrics. Primary results reveal that while core reasoning abilities are converging among top models, alignment-related behaviors diverge dramatically, with sycophancy resistance scores ranging from a perfect 1.00 for Claude-opus-4.1 to a low of 0.25 for Grok-4. | Bootstrapping Task Spaces for Self-Improvement (Read more on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04575) or [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.04575))| Yoram Bachrach, Andrei Lupu, Minqi Jiang | The paper introduces Exploratory Iteration (EXIT), an autocurriculum reinforcement learning framework that trains LLMs for multi-step self-improvement at inference-time by dynamically generating and prioritizing single-step iteration tasks from the model's own previous solution attempts. The main objective is to develop an efficient method to train LLMs for iterative refinement without the high cost and arbitrary depth limits of training on full, fixed-length improvement sequences. The key methodology, EXIT, uses Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to train a policy on a dynamically growing task buffer; this buffer is populated by treating intermediate solution attempts as new starting points and is prioritized for sampling based on the variance of returns, which serves as a learnability metric. The primary result is that across competition math, tool-use, and ML engineering domains, policies trained with EXIT variants exhibit superior inference-time self-improvement; specifically, on a competition math benchmark, the full EXIT method achieved a +2.0 percentage point net improvement in accuracy over 16 steps, compared to a +1.1 point improvement for the standard GRPO baseline. For AI practitioners, the principal implication is that EXIT provides a compute-equivalent method for fine-tuning LLMs for iterative tasks, like coding within a search scaffold, by leveraging self-generated data to create a natural curriculum that enhances both initial solution quality and the model's capacity for refinement. |
